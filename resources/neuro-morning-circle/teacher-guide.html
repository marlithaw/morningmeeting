Neuro-Regulated Circle Teacher Guide

Four 15-minute lessons, three calibrated grade bands, one six-phase regulation arc.

Lesson 1: Emotional Temperature CheckLesson 2: Mindful BreathingLesson 3: Cultural Wisdom & Shared StoriesLesson 4: Daily Intention Setting
Timing: Regulate 2 · Orient 2 · Connect 3 · Practice 3 · Commit 3 · Transition 2 = 15 minutes. Run the same arc every morning so the routine itself becomes predictable and safe.

The non-negotiable sequence

1 · Regulate the biology

“De-escalate before you educate.”

Somatic co-regulation opens the Window of Tolerance so the prefrontal cortex can access learning.

2 · Relate with warmth

“Every child welcomed and seen.”

Unconditional positive regard first — connection is the intervention, not the reward.

3 · Reason as a Warm Demander

“Absolute, neutral accountability.”

Apply boundaries calmly as procedures, never as personal attacks or public shame.

Body Weather Report

Open slidesPrint toolkit
Emotional Temperature CheckValue: SafeBrainstem & sensory-motor

I can: I can notice my “body weather” and know that every feeling is safe in our circle.

Core somatic goal: Connect internal physical arousal to relatable weather symbols to self-identify stress.

Somatic set-up: Sit with a tall spine, both hands flat on the tummy. Close eyes or look at shoes. “Breathe into your hands. Feel your tummy rise and fall. Is it warm and calm like a sunny day, fluttery like a wind gust, or tight like a stormy cloud?”

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Settle in, hands on your belly. Breathe in soft and slow… let it out like wind. Let’s do three tummy breaths.”Places hands on stomach; sits quietly and mimics breathing.If energy is high or squirmy, then do not publicly call out. Extend the breathing sways to align heart rates.
Orient
2 minutes		“Today we check our Body Weather. At Matchbook, our circle is Safe. Weather is just weather—it is okay to feel stormy here.”Points to the weather chart on the board (Sun, Clouds, Rain, Storm).If a student refuses to look up, then protect their space. Use a soft, non-demanding vocal tone.
Connect
3 minutes		“I’m going to pass our talking ball. When you get it, say your body weather: ‘My weather is [sunny / cloudy / stormy].’ You can also pass.”Receives ball, speaks weather zone using visual supports, or passes ball.If a student passes, then say: “Thank you for passing. Your silence is safe here.” Protect emotional safety first.
Practice
3 minutes		“Stormy weather can make our bodies shake. Let’s shake our hands fast for 5 seconds, then freeze and melt like a snowman.”Shakes hands vigorously, then freezes completely, releasing muscle tension.If a student crashes or screams during the shake, then do a calm reset: “My bad, I didn’t show the freeze signal. Settle down. Let’s try again.”
Commit
3 minutes		“Today, I commit to being Safe. If my weather turns stormy, I can draw a picture of a sun on my paper. Whisper your safe commitment.”Whispers commitment to a partner or draws weather state.If vague (“I’ll be good”), then reframe: “Safe means keeping hands to ourselves. Can you commit to hands in lap?”
Transition
2 minutes		“We are ready to learn. Stand on the one. Move like a slow cloud to your table for our drawing task.”Chants “On the one!” and walks slowly, keeping a regulated physical pace.If running or desk-touching occurs, then reset the transition silently with a hand cue. Re-practice the cloud-walk.
Warm Demander guardrail: Warm Demander logic balances unconditional positive regard with absolute procedural accountability. When a student is pushed outside the Window of Tolerance, de-escalate the biology (Regulate) before addressing accountability (Reason).

Safety Thermometer & Early Warning Signals

Open slidesPrint toolkit
Emotional Temperature CheckValue: SafeLimbic & emotional-identification

I can: I can find my early warning signals and read my Safety Thermometer before I boil over.

Core somatic goal: Develop internal interoception by mapping early physiological distress markers (heart rate, muscle tension, butterflies).

Somatic set-up: Place two fingers on the neck or wrist to locate the pulse. Close eyes and count beats for 10 seconds. “Is your heartbeat steady and cool, or is it racing up the thermometer?”

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Locate your pulse. Settle your breath. Let’s count our heartbeat… [pause 10s]. Breathe out all the way. Settle.”Tracks pulse, sitting upright with feet flat on the floor.If a pulse cannot be found, then say: “Place your hand flat on your chest instead. Just feel the rise of your breath.”
Orient
2 minutes		“Today we build our Coping Toolkit. At Matchbook, we are Safe. Safe means knowing our Early Warning Signals before we boil over.”Reads the Safety Thermometer board (Blue, Green, Yellow, Red).If students joke about the thermometer, then reframe: “Our bodies are biological. Thermometers measure real heat. Let’s learn our biology.”
Connect
3 minutes		“What is an early warning signal in your body when you feel stressed? ‘When I am stressed, my [heart races / tummy feels tight].’ Share.”Shares physical indicators with a partner using the target sentence frame.If major behavior issues are shared, then redirect to routine physical signals to keep it safe and low-stakes.
Practice
3 minutes		“Let’s practice the Cool-Down Check. Tighten your fists as hard as you can for 5 seconds… now open your hands and let them go loose.”Tenses facial and hand muscles, then completely relaxes them, releasing tension.If students throw hands or make contact, then do-over: “My bad, I didn’t specify personal space. Reset. Keep hands in your lap.”
Commit
3 minutes		“Write on your Toolkit slip: ‘If my heart rate goes up, I will…’ Pin it to your commitment board.”Writes a specific, observable coping commitment and posts it.If vague (“I’ll try to be calm”), then coach: “What tool will you use? ‘I will take three deep breaths.’ Write that tool down.”
Transition
2 minutes		“We carry our steady heartbeat into the day. Stand on the one.”Transitions instantly on the verbal cue, moving silently.If chatting occurs, then callback: “Check your thermometer. Settle your voice before taking your seat.”
Warm Demander guardrail: Warm Demander logic balances unconditional positive regard with absolute procedural accountability. When a student is pushed outside the Window of Tolerance, de-escalate the biology (Regulate) before addressing accountability (Reason).

Five-Finger Breathing Beats

Open slidesPrint toolkit
Mindful BreathingValue: SafeBrainstem & sensory-motor

I can: I can use my breathing hand to slow my body down whenever my engine runs too fast.

Core somatic goal: Use multi-sensory bilateral touch to slow the rate of respiration and quiet the autonomic nervous system.

Somatic set-up: Hold out one hand like a star. Using the index finger of the other hand, trace up the thumb on the inhale, pause at the tip, and trace down on the exhale. Repeat for all five fingers.

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Hold your breathing hand up high. Trace up… breathe in. Trace down… breathe out. Feel your heart slow down.”Sits tall, traces the hand, focuses on tactile input and slow breath.If tracing too fast, then physically model slower tracing. Say: “Breathe slowly, like a feather drifting down.”
Orient
2 minutes		“Our value is Safe. Our breathing hand is a tool we always carry in our pocket. We can use it anytime our engine runs too fast.”Repeats the definition of safe: keeping ourselves and our friends from harm.If a student is crying, then do not address it publicly. Keep leading the trace to co-regulate the room.
Connect
3 minutes		“Who does your hand remind you of? Share with your partner: ‘My hand makes me think of [name / my dog / a star].’”Turns to a partner and shares using the simplified sentence starter.If a student struggles with speech or words, then invite them to show their star hand or draw the person’s initial.
Practice
3 minutes		“Let’s walk in our spot. Lift your knees slow. Trace up as you lift your left knee, down as you lift your right.”Practices bilateral gross-motor coordination integrated with breath.If balance is lost or squirming starts, then simplify: “Sit down and let’s trace our toes instead. Slow and steady.”
Commit
3 minutes		“Draw a quick hand-star on your sticky note. Place it on your desk as your commitment to use your breathing tool today.”Traces the hand or draws a star on a sticky note, posting it on their workspace.If a student gets frustrated and tears their note, then respond neutrally: “Paper can rip. Let’s get a fresh one. Settle first.”
Transition
2 minutes		“Bring your calm hands with you to your seats. Stand on the one.”Stands instantly and transitions to their seats without vocalization.If chatting starts, then callback the trace: “Trace your index finger once… let’s sit in silence and begin.”

Calm-Down Sound Anchor

Open slidesPrint toolkit
Mindful BreathingValue: SafeLimbic & emotional-identification

I can: I can use a calming sound to stretch my exhale and quiet my brain’s smoke detector.

Core somatic goal: Use a calming auditory anchor to extend exhalations and settle hyperaroused brain activity (amygdala).

Somatic set-up: Play a short calming, kid-friendly clip (soft music or nature sounds — see the linked clip). Close eyes and listen, letting each exhale grow longer as the sound plays. When it ends, raise a hand and notice how your body feels.

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Close your eyes. Listen to our calming sound. Let each breath out grow longer than the one before. Inhale rise… exhale slow.”Sits in calm silence, listening to the clip with eyes closed.If a student is sensitive to the sound, then lower the volume or let them focus on breathing sways instead.
Orient
2 minutes		“Our value is Safe. A calming sound is our anchor. When we focus on it, we quiet our brain’s smoke detector.”Looks at the brain diagram on the board showing the Amygdala.If students are hyperactive or talkative, then sit in silence for a moment before the clip. Let the quiet co-regulate first.
Connect
3 minutes		“With your partner, share: what helped you stay focused on the sound? ‘The [music / quiet / slow beat] helped me stay anchored.’”Discusses sensory-motor focus strategies with a circle partner.If they drop the sentence frame, then pause and re-model: “Great thought — let’s lock it into the frame.”
Practice
3 minutes		“Now we breathe with the sound. Inhale as it rises, and stretch a long, quiet exhale as it fades. No talking—just breath.”Matches slow breathing to the rise and fall of the calming clip.If breathing turns into silly noises, then stop: “My bad, I didn’t model the quiet breath. Stop. Settle. Slow exhale with me. Go.”
Commit
3 minutes		“Write on a sticky note: ‘Today I will stay in my Window of Tolerance by…’ and post it on our circle board.”Writes a specific commitment incorporating a breathing or listening anchor.If short on time, then skip writing and do a verbal circle commitment to preserve pacing and transition.
Transition
2 minutes		“Move silently, like the last of our calming sound fading out. Stand on the one.”Stands and transitions silently, carrying a calm physical posture.If clattering or noise occurs, then callback the sound: “Just like the sound faded to quiet… let’s move in silence. Ready.”

Box Breathing

Open slidesPrint toolkit
Mindful BreathingValue: SafeSelf-awareness & respectful independence

I can: I can use box breathing to slow my heart rate and calm my body when I feel stressed.

Core somatic goal: Use a steady, even breathing pattern (box breathing) to calm the body and mind.

Somatic set-up: Trace a square in the air or on the desk. Breathe in for 4 as you trace up, hold for 4 across the top, breathe out for 4 down, hold for 4 across the bottom. Repeat four times.

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Let’s breathe in a square. Trace up—breathe in, 2, 3, 4. Hold across, 2, 3, 4. Down—breathe out, 2, 3, 4. Hold, 2, 3, 4.”Traces the square and matches the breathing count.If students rush the count, then slow your own voice and trace slower. Keep it steady.
Orient
2 minutes		“Our value is Safe. Box breathing is a tool you can use anytime—before a test, after a conflict, or when your body feels too fast.”Follows the box-breathing diagram on the board.If the room is high-energy, then add another round of breathing before moving on. Don’t rush.
Connect
3 minutes		“How did your body feel after box breathing? ‘After box breathing, my body felt ___ (steadier / calmer / slower).’ Share with a partner.”Shares how their body felt using the sentence frame.If students joke about the frame, then reframe: “This is practice. Rehearsing it now makes it real later. Use the frame.”
Practice
3 minutes		“Let’s do one more round on our own. Place a hand on your chest and feel your breathing slow down.”Runs one round of box breathing independently, hand on chest.If students get restless, then shorten to two rounds and keep the pace calm.
Commit
3 minutes		“On your card, write: ‘I will use box breathing when I ___.’ Name a real moment today.”Writes a specific moment when they will use box breathing.If a student writes a joke, then hand a fresh card: “We own our commitments here. Settle and write a real one.”
Transition
2 minutes		“Bring that calm with you. Stand on the one.”Stands and moves calmly and quietly on the cue.If the transition is loud, then reset: “Reset—too much noise. Stand on the one. Move calm. Go.”

The Kindness Echo

Open slidesPrint toolkit
Cultural Wisdom & Shared StoriesValue: RespectfulBrainstem & sensory-motor

I can: I can send a kind echo and show respect so everyone knows they belong in our circle.

Core somatic goal: Settle the social-emotional nervous system through the vocal resonance of warm, rhythmic peer affirmations.

Somatic set-up: Place the right hand over the chest to feel the heartbeat. Turn the chin to the left and take a deep breath, then to the right and exhale. Model a warm, relaxed facial expression.

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Hand on your heart. Breathe in warm feelings… breathe out kind words. Feel your heart beat in your chest.”Keeps a hand on the chest, sways gently side-to-side, stabilizing breath.If a student is poking a neighbor, then use a non-verbal prompt. Separate seats quietly without a verbal lecture.
Orient
2 minutes		“At Matchbook, we are Respectful. Respectful means protecting dignity and voice. Today we practice ‘The Kindness Echo.’”Listens to the definition of Respectful: protecting our classroom’s shared voice.If a student mimics or laughs, then reframe neutrally: “Dignity means our words make others feel strong. Let’s practice that.”
Connect
3 minutes		“I will whisper a kind word to Partner A. Partner A echoes it to Partner B. Echo around: ‘You belong in our circle.’”Whispers the kind echo to a peer, using passing gestures.If a student is selectively mute or highly anxious, then allow them to pass a gentle thumbs-up echo instead.
Practice
3 minutes		“Let’s practice the Respectful Look. Eyes to shoes, now slow eyes to your partner. Soft smile. Turn your shoulders. Echo.”Practices turning shoulders fully and making brief, comfortable eye contact.If a student turns completely away, then do not force contact. Commit
3 minutes		“Today, I commit to being Respectful by… whisper your kind commitment to our echo partner.”Whispers a commitment: “I will use kind words when someone drops their pencil.”If the commitment is too general, then guide: “What is one kind word you can use today? Yes — ‘Thank you.’ Commit to that.”
Transition
2 minutes		“Take that respect with you. Stand on the one. Walk like a quiet helper. Go.”Stands and transitions to their seats, carrying a posture of respect.If students clump or push, then reset the circle transition: “My bad, I didn’t guide the spacing. Let’s retry.”

Core somatic goal: Settle interpersonal arousal and build limbic connection through perspective-taking.

Regulate

I can: I can name what’s draining my focus and choose a tool and a next step to stay on track.

Core somatic goal: Notice what is affecting focus — sleep, hunger, stress — and choose a tool plus a specific next step.

Somatic set-up: Do a quick 5-senses reset. Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you can taste. Then take one slow breath.

PhaseTeacher script & actionStudent actionIf / Then coaching scenario
Regulate
2 minutes		“Let’s ground with our senses. Name 5 things you see… 4 you can touch… 3 you hear. Breathe and come back to right now.”Runs the 5-senses reset, anchoring attention to the present.If students get silly, then guide directly: “Hands on your desk. Feel it. Breathe. Focus.”
Orient
2 minutes		“At Matchbook, we are Responsible. Responsible means owning your next step—even on a hard day. First, know what’s draining your focus.”Reads the “What’s draining my focus?” list (sleep, food, stress, conflict).If a student mentions hunger or a hard home situation, then respond with warmth and route them privately to support.
Connect
3 minutes		“What’s one thing draining your focus today? ‘My focus is low today because ___.’ Share with a partner.”Names a personal drain on focus using the sentence frame.If a student is quiet or anxious, then let them write it on a card and hand it to you instead.
Practice
3 minutes		“Now pick a tool. If you’re tired, stretch. If you’re restless, box breathe. If you’re stuck, break the task into one small step. Rehearse your pick.”Chooses and rehearses a specific tool from their toolkit.If students go big or loud, then redo: “Tools are calm and controlled. Try again.”
Commit
3 minutes		“On your card, write: ‘Today I will own my next step by ___.’ Name one small, real win.”Writes a specific, doable next-step commitment.If too general, then prompt: “Name the exact win—finish the first task? Commit to that.”
Transition
2 minutes		“Take your next step into the day. Stand on the one.”Stands and transitions calmly and quietly on the cue.If students clump or chat, then a quiet hand cue. No power struggle—transition cleanly.

Restorative coaching & Warm Demander scenario bank

In a trauma-responsive school, behavioral issues are read not as willful compliance failures but as biometric signals of dysregulation (Porges, 2011). When a student is pushed outside the Window of Tolerance, de-escalate the biology before addressing accountability.

Trigger / behaviorRecommended trackSomatic fix
Regulatory rupture / behavioral crisis (fight / flight / freeze)Quarter 1 · Physiological RegulationPrioritize brainstem stabilization with somatic breathing, body scans, and sensory grounding. Higher-order reasoning is biologically offline until arousal settles.
Relational conflict / social harm (excluding, mocking peers)Quarter 2 · Interpersonal Harm RepairTarget limbic needs for co-regulation. Restore relational safety through perspective-taking, kindness sways, and active-listening frames.
Rigor gap / task avoidance (slamming books, head on desk)Quarter 3 · Executive Function BlueprintAddress lost prefrontal focus from allostatic load. Normalize the struggle, reframe mistakes as data, break tasks into checked wins.
Disengagement / systemic frustration (defiant head down)Quarter 4 · Student-Led Power SharingRestore agency and counter learned helplessness by transferring managing roles (Speaker, Listener) to students.
Scenario 1 · The shut-down student

Head dropped on folded arms

What backfires: Standing over the desk, pointing, demanding “Head up. Get to work.” This violates space and pushes a freezing student toward fight-or-flight.

What lands: Quietly place a sticky note: “Start task 1 when you can. I am right here.” Walk away, let the freeze thaw, and return in 10 minutes. If started, whisper “I see you working, thank you.” If still frozen, “I’ll check in during our transition. Let’s take a breath.” Follow up privately with Regulate–Relate–Reason.

Scenario 2 · The physical outburst

Knocking over a desk in frustration

What backfires: Yelling “Get out! That’s a detention! Go to the office!” This escalates public shame and feeds “the world is not safe.”

What lands: Approach calmly, supportive but firm: “Are you okay? [Pause.] Knocking over furniture breaks our safety rule, so that is a detention. I’ll write the pass, you take a deep breath, and we’ll talk at lunch about repairing our classroom.” Apply the boundary neutrally, pivot to regulation, and schedule the restorative repair.

Same-day response

Protect safety first

If a student shares a current concern of harm, hunger, or crisis, respond with warm empathy, protect privacy, do not process it in front of peers, and route the student privately to support services the same day.

Sources and implementation notes

This universal Tier 1 block is grounded in Lori Desautels’ brain-aligned, trauma-responsive practices and Stephen Porges’ Polyvagal Theory, and it maps to Matchbook’s ROAR values — Safe, Respectful, Responsible. Quarter 1 protects psychological safety through predictable, sensory-motor co-regulation before instructional pressure begins.
