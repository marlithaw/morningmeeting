Matchbook Morning Meeting · Gratitude in Action · Thankful TogetherGrades 3–5 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. Gratitude is not only something we say. It is something we do. Today we find ways to show thanks with our actions. Today’s focus is Gratitude in Action.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for today: we talk about actions anyone can take. No one is called out for what they did not do.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes. Then read the third card aloud: “Helping hands ready.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can show gratitude through actions that help, take care of shared things, and include others.” Point to each picture and read its label. Ask students to read the goal with you. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Malik, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: How does the student in the video show gratitude?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Kids with character: Gratitude, GreatSchools). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Lucia, how does the student in the video show gratitude?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “After indoor recess, games are on the floor. A new student is sitting alone at lunch. The class library books are stacked in a messy pile.” Then read each question: What gratitude action fits each situation? Which action would mean the most, and why? Give ten seconds of silent think time. Turn and talk: “Partner A answers the first question. Partner B answers the second. Go.” After 30 seconds, say “Switch.” Equity stick: pull two sticks. Ask Andre, “What did your partner say?” Ask Sofia, “Do you agree or want to add on? Say more.” Close with the slide line: “Thankful for it? Take care of it. Thankful for them? Include them.” Strong response: A strong answer matches an action to each situation (put games away, invite the new student to sit with you, shelve the books) and gives a reason, like “Inviting the new student matters most because being alone at lunch can feel bad all day.”

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Gratitude in Action Plan page from the 3–5 toolkit. Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step in one sentence, then release. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Responsible: “Sofia, you put the games away without being asked. That’s gratitude in action.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The student matches a fitting action to each situation, explains which matters most, and plans one specific action for today. Access: Grade 3: match actions using the chips. Grades 4–5: plan an action and predict how it will help someone. Students may draw and dictate.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Actions Speak. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “My gratitude action today will be putting the class library back in order, for everyone who reads there.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Today, do one gratitude action no one asked you to do.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If a student says, “Why should I clean up what someone else left?” answer once: “Because it’s our room. Taking care of it is how we say thanks for it.” Then move on and award points to students who help.
