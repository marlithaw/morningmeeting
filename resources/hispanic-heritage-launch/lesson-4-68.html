Slide 1 · Launch · 0:30

Today our question is: How do stories and the way we listen make room for people? You can think, talk, draw, or listen. We will learn something and practice one way to use it.

Facilitation

Read the title and essential question. Tell students that listening and passing are welcome forms of participation.

Look for

Students can state today’s focus in their own words.

Access and support

Allow a private written response or a pass. Give students room to question a claim and support it with evidence. Invite personal connections without requesting family histories or assigning anyone an identity.

Slide 3 · Orient · 2:00

Pura Belpré began work as the New York Public Library’s first Puerto Rican librarian in 1921. She shared Puerto Rican folktales through storytelling and published books. We can use those facts to consider whose stories readers can access. Today, propose an addition to a classroom display that would expand what we learn. Name a specific question and a source rather than assuming one story represents a whole community.

Facilitation

Read the student-facing cards and use the script. Pause for one check: “What did Belpré share through her library work?” Background for teacher reference: Pura Belpré began work at the New York Public Library in 1921 as its first Puerto Rican librarian. She shared Puerto Rican folktales with children and published stories. Connect that documented work to today’s question about listening and access to stories. Students may discuss a book, invent a story, or pass. Personal heritage sharing and family interviews are not required. The class can learn from published sources without placing the burden on classmates.

Look for

Students identify the main concept and can name a supporting detail.

Access and support

Allow a private written response or a pass. Give students room to question a claim and support it with evidence. Invite personal connections without requesting family histories or assigning anyone an identity.

Take a quiet moment with this question. You may use a classroom example, a book, or an imagined situation. Partner A has a turn, then Partner B. A pass is okay.

Facilitation

Give 10 seconds to think, 20 seconds per partner, and time for one voluntary response. Do not invite the class to guess someone’s culture or family practices.

Look for

Slide 6 · Practice · 4:00

Here is our practice situation. A class display features only one kind of story about Hispanic and Latino communities. Design a display addition. Explain what students would learn and where you would look. We will explain our choices, try them, and make them clearer.

Facilitation

Minute 1: read and model. Minutes 2–3: Pairs draft a display addition with a topic, a specific learning question, and a source plan such as a library catalog or museum collection. No one needs to disclose family history. Each pair explains how the addition avoids treating one story as universal. Minute 4: hear one example and use this answer guidance to clarify and rehearse once more: An addition could explore another Puerto Rican author, a scientist’s firsthand account, or different genres by Latino writers. Students must state what they would investigate, not invent a fact or claim that one author represents everyone.

Look for

An addition could explore another Puerto Rican author, a scientist’s firsthand account, or different genres by Latino writers. Students must state what they would investigate, not invent a fact or claim that one author represents everyone.
