Slide 3 · Orient · 2:00

Ellen Ochoa became the first Hispanic woman to travel to space in 1993 as a NASA astronaut. Roberto Clemente, a baseball player from Puerto Rico, also used his success to help people in need. These facts show different contributions. When we recognize someone, naming what they did helps us understand more than simply calling them famous.

Facilitation

Read the student-facing cards and use the script. Pause for one check: “What did each person contribute?” Background for teacher reference: Introduce each person with the source-backed profile, without invented quotations. Ellen Ochoa became the first Hispanic woman in space in 1993 as a NASA astronaut. Roberto Clemente was born in Puerto Rico and was both a baseball player and a humanitarian who used his success to support others. These are two examples among many contributions, not representatives of every Hispanic or Latino experience. Treat the discussion of what students find inspiring as their interpretation.

Look for

Students identify the main concept and can name a supporting detail.

Access and support

Offer the sentence frame in speech and writing. Give quiet thinking time before partner talk. Students may pass or use a fictional example. Ask for one specific detail to support an answer.

Slide 6 · Practice · 3:00

Here is our practice situation. A class display needs a sentence about each person’s contribution. Improve the vague sentence using a fact. Then plan one helpful action. We will explain our choices, try them, and make them clearer.

Facilitation

Minute 1: model the prompt. Minute 2: partners rehearse or respond. Minute 3: hear one example and clarify. A stronger Clemente caption names his baseball career and humanitarian work. A contribution claim should identify an action, not just praise someone as “great.”

Look for
