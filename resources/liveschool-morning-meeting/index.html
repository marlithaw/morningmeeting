Effort, Feedback & Growth

Each version includes 10 slides and teacher notes. Use the arrow buttons or arrow keys to advance. Press N for notes. Use your browser’s full-screen view for presenting.

Notes appear on the same screen when opened. Review them before projecting, or keep this guide on a separate teacher device.

Before The Meeting

  1. Open LiveSchool in a separate tab using a school-approved demonstration account or a de-identified example. Avoid projecting classmates’ personal behavior records.
  2. Preview the school’s green/red behavior labels, student Timeline, and available balance. Confirm whether the school deducts negative points from spending balances so you can explain the actual setting.
  3. Prepare to switch between these slides and the live platform during slides 5–7. Students can watch and direct your clicks. Individual logins are optional.
  4. Use the confirmed school store details: it operates during lunch periods and school counselors run it. Follow the school’s announced visit directions.
Open educator LiveSchool Open student LiveSchool

What Students Will Understand

Green points record positive behaviors. Red points record behavior concerns to reflect on and practice. The Timeline helps explain individual entries. The available balance is the place to check spending points.

Our school store acknowledges students’ hard work and growth. Counselors run the schoolwide store during lunch periods. A student can ask a teacher about feedback and a counselor about the store process.

Use concrete examples of effort, improvement, and practicing Safe, Respectful, Responsible. Keep commitments focused on actions. A point total does not define a student’s worth.

Grades K–2 · Complete Teacher Script

Read each label aloud. Let children point, gesture, or echo a phrase. Keep the walkthrough teacher-led. Students do not need their own devices.

1. My effort. My growth. · Launch · 0:30
Today we will explore LiveSchool together. We will find point information, talk about green and red points, and connect our hard work and growth to our school store.

Teacher move: Before projecting, open a school-approved demonstration account or a de-identified example. Have the student view ready in a separate tab. Preview which points and labels your school displays.

Look for: Students know this is a guided practice with feedback, not a comparison of classmates.

2. Ready to learn · Regulate · 0:30
Let us settle before we look at feedback. Take two comfortable breaths with me. You may keep breathing normally and simply feel your feet on the floor.

Teacher move: Model two gentle breaths. Offer a quiet gaze or feet-on-floor option.

Look for: Bodies settle without requiring everyone to use the same posture.

3. Our LiveSchool walkthrough · Orient · 1:00
A point records information about a choice. We use that information to notice what is working and what needs practice. Your worth and your place in our community do not depend on a number.

Teacher move: Introduce the route. Explain that teacher and student screens can look different. Everyone can follow by watching.

Look for: Students can name what they will learn to find.

4. Hard work takes practice · Connect · 1:00
Think of a time you kept working, asked for help, or tried again. What changed because you practiced? We want to notice that kind of growth at school.

Teacher move: Give 15 seconds to think, then a brief partner turn. Accept an example outside school. Invite one response about effort.

Look for: Students name a specific effort or improvement rather than a point total.

5. Green and red points · Practice · Points · 2:00
Green points record positive behaviors adults recognize. Red points record behavior concerns that need attention or practice. We look at the reason for an entry. A red entry is a moment to understand and respond to. You can still make a helpful next choice.

Teacher move: In the teacher Points screen, show only a demonstration record. Identify the green and red values and the selected timeframe. Read an actual school behavior label for each color. If using student view only, locate a positive and negative Timeline entry. Do not promise that every student screen shows teacher-style point bubbles.

Look for: Students connect each color to recorded behavior and can describe a constructive response.

6. The story behind a point · Practice · Timeline · 2:00
Now we will look at the information behind a point. Read the behavior, the date, and any comment that appears. A comment can help explain what the adult noticed. If you are confused, ask the teacher privately and respectfully.

Teacher move: Switch to the prepared student view. Open Timeline. Locate one example and read the visible date, behavior, point value, and comment if present. Return to the main menu or dashboard. If no comment appears, explain that comments are optional. Ask a student to describe the route back to that entry.

Look for: Students can find the entry and use its details before making an assumption.

7. Points and spending balance · Practice · Balance · 3:00
Our point history shows recorded choices. Our spending balance tells us what is available to use. These numbers may differ. We read the balance on the screen rather than guessing from the green number.

Teacher move: Point to the balance in student view. Explain the school’s verified rule for how red points affect spending. LiveSchool lets schools choose whether negative points reduce the balance, so confirm this before teaching. In the teacher view, the green/red timeframe can change while the saved balance remains separate. Model returning to the dashboard, finding the balance, and reopening Timeline. Have students direct your clicks or practice on their own approved account. Do not open the store catalog or make a purchase.

Look for: Students identify the available balance and can distinguish it from green/red history.

8. Why we have a school store · Practice · School store · 2:00
The school store is one way our school acknowledges the effort you put into learning, practicing our expectations, and growing. Our school counselors run it during lunch periods. We want you to understand the work behind the points and feel proud of your progress.

Teacher move: Explain only the confirmed arrangement: schoolwide store, lunch periods, school counselors. Tell students to follow the announced visit directions and ask a counselor for help with the store process. Do not invent open days, thresholds, deduction rules, or eligibility conditions. Ask students to connect a real action to growth.

Look for: Students explain that the store acknowledges effort and growth and identify counselors as the adults who run it.

9. My next choice · Commit · 2:00
In this example, what needed to change? What showed growth afterward? Choose one action you can practice today. Make it something you can actually do, even when the moment is hard.

Teacher move: Allow a short partner response to the scenario. Give students time to say, write, or point to a commitment. If they say “get more points,” ask what action they will practice. Avoid promising a point for every action.

Look for: Each commitment names an observable behavior rather than a score target.

10. Ready for the next lesson · Transition · 1:00
Show me or tell me how you would find your feedback. Tell me why we have the school store. Carry your commitment into our next lesson. Your next choice is another chance to practice.

Teacher move: Take two brief responses. Clarify: Timeline helps explain points, balance shows available spending points, and counselors run the store during lunch to acknowledge hard work and growth. Have students sign out or close their own account as appropriate and transition to the next task.

Look for: Students can locate feedback, explain the store’s purpose, and begin the next routine.
