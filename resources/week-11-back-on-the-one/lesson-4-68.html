Matchbook Morning Meeting · Back from Break. Ready to Learn.Grades 6–8 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Reconnect to Monday’s learning goal. Ask students to hold it in mind or point to their card. Today: I can use evidence to plan a realistic next week of learning. Give one real example from yesterday if observed; do not invent a success. Use the usual greeting. The recurring video may play before the timed meeting. No one has to share a break story.

Regulate · 2:00

Model the familiar reset without demanding a particular body posture or eye contact. Invite three comfortable breaths, or quiet noticing for students who prefer it. Then point to the first learning material. Use the existing Relax resource if helpful within these two minutes. Keep established individual supports available.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “I can use evidence to plan a realistic next week of learning.” Point to each visual and model its action briefly. Connect it to an actual lesson today. For Monday, return the pre-break Comeback Card if available; students without one choose a goal now. Our aim is repeated learning practice over the next month, not a promise to be perfect. Check: ask a student to name or show one action.

Connect · See It · 2:00

Read and model both examples. They are fictional learning examples, not reports about students. Give 20 seconds to look. Ask: Why is this a stronger plan than “work harder next week”? Worked response: The learner keeps a useful strategy, adds a specific adjustment, and names evidence and a review moment. Two attempts guide the next step; they do not establish a month-long trend. Do not accept only “the second one is better”; point to the action or feature that makes it useful.

Connect · Name It · 1:00

Give ten seconds of thinking. Partner A responds, then B. Accept pointing, oral response, writing, or a pass from public sharing. Name the learning move explicitly: The learner keeps a useful strategy, adds a specific adjustment, and names evidence and a review moment. Two attempts guide the next step; they do not establish a month-long trend.

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Model the fictional evidence and decision. Students identify the link. Students privately review a real attempt or a starting-point sample made today. In pairs, explain a keep/adjust/support decision using the sample. Record the next class practice opportunity and a brief weekly check. The teacher selects an existing class time for the checks; do not create a new meeting requirement. 0:00–0:30: model. 0:30–1:00: name the evidence link. 1:00–2:00: review and decide. 2:00–3:00: partner challenge and schedule. Look for: The next-week plan cites work, identifies a controllable action or needed support, and sets a review opportunity. Access: Grade 6: use the evidence–decision–next-step frame. Grades 7–8: distinguish strategy use from achievement and specify the support needed. No public grade comparison; the work stays with the learner.

Commit · 3:00

Use the matching toolkit card. Model: “The evidence shows a claim without support. I will mark evidence before drafting in science, then compare two explanations at next week’s check.” Give 90 seconds to record, 45 seconds to check that the action fits the goal, and 45 seconds to name when it will happen. Keep goals private if preferred. Keep the same goal card across the next month. Suggested brief checks: October 22 and 29, November 5 and 12, during an existing class routine. Adjust to the actual school schedule; these are proposed review points, not new scheduled events.

Transition · 2:00

Name the actual next task and show where to start. Use the established classroom release cue. Students begin the planned action with their usual supports. Notice specific learning behavior rather than judging mood, speed, or stillness. Revisit the goal briefly during class; the approved Ember boards remain unchanged.
