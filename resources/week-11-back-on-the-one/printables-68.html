Monday October 19 · Grades 6–8

Our Place. Our People.

The moment

Nia returns quietly. Jay asks, “Tell us everything!”

A different move

Jay tries: “Good morning, Nia. Your place is here.”

What changes

Nia chooses a wave. Jay accepts her choice.

See it / Name it

How did Jay show welcome without making Nia perform happiness?

Worked response: A welcome offers belonging, practical access, and choice without demanding disclosure.

Do it

Reentry Design Lab: pairs rewrite “Tell everybody what you did!” into two low-pressure welcomes. Role-play a quiet return, then a later arrival. The returning student chooses greeting, space, or a private adult check-in. Switch roles and revise the invitation.

My commitment

At ____, I will make returning easier by ____.

Say, point, draw, dictate, or write. Private stories stay private.

Tuesday October 20 · Grades 6–8

Listen for the Whole Beat

The moment

Ari gives two steps. Bo starts answering before Ari finishes.

A different move

Bo pauses: “Let me check what I heard.”

What changes

Ari confirms. Bo responds to the whole message.

See it / Name it

How did verification improve the accuracy of the final drawing?

Worked response: The listener distinguishes what was said from what was inferred.

Do it

Signal vs. Assumption: A describes a red circle containing a dot, a blue square below, and a line connecting them. B waits, paraphrases, and asks one precise question before drawing. Switch with a triangle above two parallel lines. Identify which clarification prevented an error.

My commitment

In ____ today, I will paraphrase ____ before I ____.

Say, point, draw, dictate, or write. Private stories stay private.

Wednesday October 21 · Grades 6–8

A Reset Is a Way Back In

The moment

Sam says, “I missed the first step.”

A different move

An adult offers: “Let me show you one part.”

What changes

Sam tries that part with support.

See it / Name it

Why was “circle the first word” a usable reentry step?

Worked response: Rejoining includes a workable support and a visible first action; a second check-in is named if needed.

Do it

Rejoin Studio: trios take learner, adult, and observer roles. Scenario: a student has missed the first direction and feels behind. Learner signals; adult offers a first-step model or read-aloud; learner attempts one part. Rotate. Observer names the support and the action, without rating feelings.

My commitment

If ____ blocks me in ____, I will ask for ____ and attempt ____.

Say, point, draw, dictate, or write. Private stories stay private.

Thursday October 22 · Grades 6–8

Make Room in the Cypher

The moment

Lee notices two voices filling the group. Ren has no turn.

A different move

Lee asks Ren, “Point, tell us, or pass?”

What changes

The group waits and uses the idea Ren offers.

See it / Name it

Where did Lee redistribute access without speaking for Ren?

Worked response: The group offers options, allows processing time, and incorporates available contributions.

Do it

Access-by-Design: groups of three solve the six-shape pattern with three separate clues: alternating circle-triangle, six shapes total, start with a circle. First round: an optional verbal contribution from each. Second: offer pointing, writing, dictation, or pass. Compare which design made the task accessible; do not rank students.

My commitment

In ____, I will offer ____ so that ____ can contribute by choice.

Say, point, draw, dictate, or write. Private stories stay private.
