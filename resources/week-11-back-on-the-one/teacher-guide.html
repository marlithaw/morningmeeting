Teacher Guide

Return to learning after fall break · differentiated K–8 instruction

Week hubTeacher guide

Teach the return to learning

This unit continues Week 10’s Comeback Card and Week 9’s First Five. It is not a greeting-skills unit. Students recover a learning goal, practice a useful move, protect their focus, use feedback, and plan how to check growth over the next month.

Design: existing Week 9 typography, color sequence, icon cards, partner frames, modeled practice, teacher notes, and transition into live classwork. Week 10 supplies the goal-card continuity and concrete student tools.

Timing: Welcome ready screen; Regulate 2, Orient 2, Connect 3, Practice 3, Commit 3, Transition 2 minutes. Use recurring videos before the timed block or within Regulate; do not add a full video on top of the practice.

Look for across the week: a strategy used, a return to a task, a revision after feedback, and an evidence-based next step. Do not use quietness, speed, mood, or public enthusiasm as the measure of learning.

Next-month follow-through: keep the goal and selected work together. Suggested checks during an existing class routine are October 22, October 29, November 5, and November 12. Verify school-day availability locally. Ask: What did I try? What does my work show? What will I keep or adjust?

If a student has no card or sample: make a starting point today. Do not make missing materials a barrier.

Monday October 19 · Grades K–2

We’re Back. Let’s Grow!

Target: I can choose one thing to learn and take my first step.

Prepare: My Comeback Goal card; pencil or drawing tools; Monday’s goal and one current learning task.

Evidence: The child starts one accessible part of a real task and can point to or name that action.

Differentiate: Kindergarten: offer two concrete materials and let the child choose. Grade 1: dictate the action. Grade 2: write the action and explain how it helps. An adult can scribe; drawing and showing count.

Welcome · ready screen

Welcome back from fall break. Before break, we made a Comeback Card with a ready body, a ready routine, and a learning goal. This week we put that plan to work. We are building the next month one useful action at a time. Use the usual greeting. The recurring video may play before the timed meeting. No one has to share a break story.

For Monday, return the pre-break Comeback Card if available; students without one choose a goal now. Our aim is repeated learning practice over the next month, not a promise to be perfect. Check: ask a student to name or show one action.

Worked response: The second learner can point and try. A wish becomes a plan when we name an action. Do not accept only “the second one is better”; point to the action or feature that makes it useful.

Connect · Name It · 1:00

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Use one familiar picture book, letter card, counting set, or current class task per learner. Model placing materials, pointing to the first part, and trying it. Children show or name the first action, then everyone rehearses with their own material. After a short pause, repeat the start. This is a start rehearsal, not a speed race. 0:00–0:30: model. 0:30–1:00: name the first action. 1:00–2:00: everyone tries. 2:00–3:00: pause, reset, and start again. Look for: The child starts one accessible part of a real task and can point to or name that action. Access: Kindergarten: offer two concrete materials and let the child choose. Grade 1: dictate the action. Grade 2: write the action and explain how it helps. An adult can scribe; drawing and showing count.
