Matchbook Morning Meeting · Everyone Belongs · Disability Awareness WeekGrades 3–5 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. Fair means everyone has a way to take part. This week is Disability Awareness Week at Matchbook. Today’s focus is Fairness and Access.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for the whole week: use the examples on the slides. No one is asked to talk about their own or a family member’s health or disability, and no one points to a classmate.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes or make eye contact. Then read the third card aloud: “You belong here.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can design a change that removes a barrier to participation.” Point to each picture and read its label. Ask students to read the goal with you. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: What helps someone participate?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Lots of Ways to Participate, Sesame Workshop). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “What helps someone participate?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “An activity only accepts spoken answers.” Then read each question: How could a student using pictures take part? Name or sketch one change. Give ten seconds of silent think time. Turn and talk: “Partner A answers the first question. Partner B answers the second. Go.” After 30 seconds, say “Switch.” Equity stick: pull two sticks. Ask the first student, “What did your partner say?” Ask the second, “Do you agree or want to add on? Say more.” Close with the slide line: “What would your change make possible?” Strong response: Accept drawing, pointing, a communication board, answer cards, or another workable response option. The change lets the student show the same thinking a different way.

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Access Detective worksheet (Disability Awareness packet, page 10). Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step in one sentence, then release. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Responsible: “You found a barrier and designed a fix. That is responsible problem solving.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The sketch or description shows a workable non-spoken response option and explains what it makes possible. Access: Grade 3: choose one of the three change cards on the Practice slide and sketch it. Grades 4–5: design an original change and name who else it helps. Accept labeled sketches.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Home Access Detective. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “Add picture answer cards. Each student holds up a card, so everyone answers at the same time and every answer counts.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Today, notice one barrier and one way to remove it.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If a student says a support is “unfair” or “cheating,” use it as a teaching moment: “Fair means everyone gets what they need to take part. Glasses are not cheating for someone who needs them.” Keep the focus on supports in general, not on any classmate’s accommodations.
