Matchbook Morning Meeting · Everyone Belongs · Disability Awareness WeekGrades K–2 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. This week is Disability Awareness Week. Every student belongs here. This week is Disability Awareness Week at Matchbook. Today’s focus is Everyone Belongs.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for the whole week: use the examples on the slides. No one is asked to talk about their own or a family member’s health or disability, and no one points to a classmate.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes or make eye contact. Then read the third card aloud: “You belong here.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can welcome someone and offer a place to join.” Point to each picture and read its label. Have students repeat each label with a hand motion. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Jaylen, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: What helps someone feel welcome?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Lots of Ways to Be Kind, Sesame Workshop). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Nia, what helps someone feel welcome?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “Someone wants to join your game.” Point to choice A, then choice B, and read each one. Say: “Think. Do not call out. Point when I say go.” Give ten seconds of think time, then say “Go.” Students point to the slide or hold up one or two fingers. Equity stick: pull a stick and ask, “Which choice did you point to?” Then pull a second stick: “Point to a welcoming choice.” Accept pointing, signing, a device, or a whispered answer to a partner. Answer: choice A, “You can play with us.” Name it: “That choice helps someone belong.” Talk move: “Who can say it in their own words?”

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Welcome Ticket worksheet (Disability Awareness packet, page 2). Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step on a copy under the document camera, or hold up the page and point. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Respectful: “Amara, you offered a real role, not just a seat. That is a respectful invitation.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The child points to or names a welcoming choice and shows one way to invite a classmate. Access: Kindergarten: point to the two picture choices on the slide, then circle on the Welcome Ticket with adult support. Grade 1: say the welcoming words aloud. Grade 2: write the welcoming words on the ticket. Drawing, pointing, and dictating all count.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: My Welcome Ticket. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “You can sit here with us. Do you want to draw or build?” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Welcome someone today: in class, at lunch, or at recess.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If a student says someone “can’t play” or laughs at the B choice, respond privately and briefly: “In our class, everyone gets an invitation. Show me the welcoming choice.” Then return to the lesson. Do not debate it in front of the group.
