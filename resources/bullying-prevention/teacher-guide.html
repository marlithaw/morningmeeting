Bullying Prevention Teacher Guide

Five 15-minute lessons, three calibrated grade bands, one shared response language.

Lesson 1: Name ItLesson 2: Know ItLesson 3: Upstander, Not BystanderLesson 4: Speak UpLesson 5: Make It Right
Timing: Greeting 2 minutes · Sharing 3 minutes · Activity 7 minutes · Message 3 minutes. The activity includes one minute for directions/reset and six minutes of core practice.

Non-negotiable response language

Student report opening

“I need to report possible bullying or harassment.”

Who · what actions · when · where · pattern · evidence · safety now.

Teacher response

“Thank you for telling me. I am going to help.”

Protect privacy. Do not promise secrecy. Follow the Matchbook Bullying Response Protocol the same day.

If harm continues

Tell again—and tell another trusted adult.

Students are not responsible for proving the case or managing retaliation alone.

0:00–2:00Greeting
Kindness Wave		Model first. Make sure every child is greeted by name; quietly pair anyone without a partner.Wave to a neighbor: “Good morning, [name]. I am glad you are here.”
2:00–5:00SharingAccept a face, gesture, or one-word answer. Do not invite personal disclosures in the full group.

5:00–12:00Activity
Three clues adults listen for		Teach: Power: One person has more size, popularity, information, access, or control. · Pattern: It happens again and again—or looks likely to keep happening. · Harm: The behavior is unwanted and hurts, scares, humiliates, or excludes.

Hold up 1 for accident, 2 for conflict/problem, or 3 for possible bullying. Then name the clue you noticed.		Analyze, sort, or role-play using the shared language.

Preview: Next: four ways bullying can happen.
Teacher guardrail: Students report what they noticed; trained adults determine whether the facts meet the formal definition of bullying. A one-time harmful or dangerous act still matters and must be addressed.
Look for: Students sort without naming real classmates and can say “tell an adult” when unsure.

Grades K–2

Materials: Stop / Support / Tell cards · Puppet or role-play cards

TimePartTeacher script + moveStudent action
0:00–2:00Greeting
Help Is Here		Model a warm voice; students may echo if the full sentence is difficult.Greet a classmate: “Good morning, [name]. You belong here.”
2:00–5:00SharingGather quick, safe ideas: stand nearby, invite, get an adult, use kind words.

5:00–12:00Activity
Three safe upstander moves		Teach: Stop: If it feels safe, use a clear voice: “Stop. That is not okay.” · Support: Stand with the person, invite them with you, or say something kind. · Tell: Get a trusted adult right away. Keep telling until someone helps.

Practice one safe move with a puppet. Students may say the words, point to a card, or ask an adult to act.		Analyze, sort, or role-play using the shared language.
  • No seat for you: Support by inviting the child; tell an adult about the repeated exclusion.
  • Pushing: Get an adult. Do not put your body in danger.
  • Mean game chat: Support the classmate and tell a trusted adult.
12:00–15:00MessageStudents show the Stop, Support, or Tell hand signal as they echo.
Class message: An upstander helps without making the danger bigger.		Commit: I can stop safely, support the person, or tell an adult.
Preview: Tomorrow: how to make a clear report.
Teacher guardrail: Never direct a child to physically intervene. Praise getting adult help as an active upstander move.
Look for: Students choose adult help for physical danger and can name at least one support move.

TimePartTeacher script + moveStudent action
0:00–2:00Greeting
Belonging Signal		Model natural language; avoid making authenticity feel scripted.Greet a neighbor by name and offer a brief belonging statement or a simple “good morning.”
2:00–5:00SharingName audience pressure, fear, status, uncertainty, and digital permanence without shaming.

Five options—choose for safety		Teach: Interrupt: Name the behavior only if it is safe. · Redirect: Change the audience, topic, or location. · Support: Check in privately and restore connection. · Document: Preserve evidence privately; never repost or solicit more harm. · Report: Give facts to an adult and escalate urgent threats immediately.

Teams choose the lowest-risk effective response. Explain what the move protects and what it avoids.		Analyze, sort, or role-play using the shared language.
  • Cafeteria audience: Reduce the audience, support the targeted student, and get adult help. Do not add another recording.
  • Anonymous account: Preserve URLs/screenshots privately and report. Do not amplify or try to expose the operator yourself.
  • Immediate danger: Report immediately to an adult. Support the target only if doing so does not delay the urgent report.
12:00–15:00MessageClose by naming that freezing is a stress response; the commitment is about the next available action.
Class message: Effective upstanders reduce the audience, increase support, preserve evidence, and bring in help.		Commit: I will choose a move that is both safe and helpful—even when no one applauds it.
Preview: Tomorrow: the exact words for a clear report.
Teacher guardrail: Never instruct students to investigate, confront a group alone, or circulate harmful content. Urgent threats bypass role-play and go directly to an adult.
Look for: Students justify strategy based on safety, audience, urgency, and likelihood of retaliation.

TimePartTeacher script + moveStudent action
0:00–2:00Greeting
Evidence, Not Popularity		Keep recognition factual. Students may pass rather than invent evidence.Greet a peer and cite one observed action connected to Safe, Respectful, or Responsible.
2:00–5:00SharingLand on impact, action, consequence, repair, boundary, and changed behavior.
Prompt: What is the difference between an apology that protects reputation and accountability that repairs harm?		Image-management focuses on ___. Accountability focuses on ___.
5:00–12:00Activity
The accountability test		Teach: Specific ownership: Names the action without excuses, minimizing, or demanding agreement. · Impact + boundary: Acknowledges harm and accepts the other person’s need for space or support. · Repair + consequence: Offers appropriate repair and does not treat repair as a way to avoid consequences. · Changed pattern: Shows different behavior over time and stops retaliation.

Analyze each response for ownership, impact, boundary, repair, and changed pattern. Then draft one pledge line.		Analyze, sort, or role-play using the shared language.
