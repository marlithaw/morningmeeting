Lesson 1 · Name It · Grades K–2

Three clues adults listen for

01

Power

One person has more size, popularity, information, access, or control.

02

Pattern

It happens again and again—or looks likely to keep happening.

03

Harm

The behavior is unwanted and hurts, scares, humiliates, or excludes.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 1

A bump

A classmate bumps your block tower by accident, says sorry, and helps rebuild it.

Teacher key: Accident—the harm was not on purpose and the student repaired it.

Cut card · Lesson 1

One ball

Two classmates both want the same ball. They argue one time and both keep trying to get it.

Teacher key: Conflict—both students have similar power and are part of the problem.

Cut card · Lesson 1

Every snack time

The same student takes another child’s snack every day, laughs, and will not stop when asked.

Teacher key: Possible bullying—there is a pattern, harm, and possible unfair power. Tell an adult.

Message/pledge: Bullying may be hurtful behavior with unfair power, a pattern, and harm. It is never okay.
Student commitment: If I am hurt, worried, or unsure, I will tell a trusted adult.
Lesson 2 · Know It · Grades K–2

Four ways harm can happen

01

Physical

Hitting, pushing, taking, or damaging things.

02

Verbal

Threats, cruel names, or hurtful words.

03

Social

Repeatedly leaving someone out or spreading rumors.

04

Cyber

Harmful messages, pictures, posts, or game/chat behavior.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 2

On the playground

A student keeps pushing the same child away from the swings.

Teacher key: Physical, and possibly social if the pushing is used to exclude.

Cut card · Lesson 2

At lunch

A group repeatedly whispers that no one should sit with one child.

Teacher key: Social and verbal.

Cut card · Lesson 2

On a tablet

Someone sends the same scary message in a game chat every day.

Teacher key: Cyber and verbal.

Message/pledge: Harm can happen to bodies, through words, inside friendships, or on screens.
Student commitment: I will name what happened and tell an adult.
Lesson 3 · Upstander, Not Bystander · Grades K–2

Three safe upstander moves

01

Stop

If it feels safe, use a clear voice: “Stop. That is not okay.”

02

Support

Stand with the person, invite them with you, or say something kind.

03

Tell

Get a trusted adult right away. Keep telling until someone helps.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 3

No seat for you

A child is repeatedly told they cannot sit with the group.

Teacher key: Support by inviting the child; tell an adult about the repeated exclusion.

Cut card · Lesson 3

Pushing

A child is being pushed and looks scared.

Teacher key: Get an adult. Do not put your body in danger.

Cut card · Lesson 3

Mean game chat

A classmate says someone keeps sending mean messages in a game.

Teacher key: Support the classmate and tell a trusted adult.

Message/pledge: An upstander helps without making the danger bigger.
Student commitment: I can stop safely, support the person, or tell an adult.
Lesson 4 · Speak Up · Grades K–2

A clear report

01

Who

Who was hurt? Who did it? Who saw it?

02

What

What happened? Use action words.

03

When + Where

When and where did it happen?

04

Again + Safe

Has it happened before? Do you feel safe right now?

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 4

Practice report

A puppet says: “The same child takes my snack at lunch every day.”

Teacher key: Combine the facts: who, what, when/where, pattern, and safety need.

Cut card · Lesson 4

If the first adult is busy

You told one adult, but you still do not feel safe.

Teacher key: Tell another trusted adult and keep telling until someone helps.

Message/pledge: Telling to keep someone safe is reporting. Reporting is brave.
Student commitment: I will say: “I need to report something. I need help staying safe.”
Lesson 5 · Make It Right · Grades K–2

Repair has four parts

01

Stop

Stop the harmful behavior.

02

Listen

Listen to the impact without arguing.

03

Own + repair

Name what you did and fix what you can.

04

Change

Show a different choice again and again.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 5

Weak apology

“Sorry you got mad. Can we be friends now?”

Teacher key: Better: “I took your things. That was wrong. I will return them and stop. You can have space.”

Cut card · Lesson 5

Class pledge

Choose one promise our class can keep every day.

Teacher key: Combine student language into one short, observable class pledge.

Message/pledge: Our class pledge: We notice harm. We speak up. We get help. We repair. Everyone belongs.
Student commitment: Today I will show our pledge by ___.
