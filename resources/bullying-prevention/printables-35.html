Lesson 1 · Name It · Grades 3–5

Power + Pattern + Harm

01

Power

A real or perceived advantage—strength, popularity, information, identity, group size, or access.

02

Pattern

Repeated behavior or behavior likely to be repeated.

03

Harm

Unwanted behavior that hurts, scares, humiliates, intimidates, or excludes.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 1

Group project

Two friends disagree once about who should present. Both interrupt and then ask the teacher for help.

Teacher key: Conflict—roughly equal power, one event, and both participate.

Cut card · Lesson 1

Lunch table

A popular group repeatedly tells one student the table is “full,” then posts jokes about the student in a group chat.

Teacher key: Possible bullying—social power, repeated exclusion, and harm are present.

Cut card · Lesson 1

Mean comment

A student insults a classmate once after losing a game.

Teacher key: It may not meet the bullying definition yet, but it is harmful behavior and still requires an adult response.

Message/pledge: Bullying involves unfair power, a pattern or likely pattern, and unwanted harm.
Student commitment: I will report the facts when I see power, pattern, or harm—or when I am unsure.
Lesson 2 · Know It · Grades 3–5

Four categories—not four separate boxes

01

Physical

Actions against a body or property.

02

Verbal

Spoken or written threats, insults, slurs, or cruel teasing.

03

Social

Rumors, exclusion, humiliation, or damaging relationships/status.

04

Cyber

Digital messages, posts, images, games, accounts, or group chats used to harm.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 2

Fake account

Students make a fake account for a classmate and post embarrassing statements in the classmate’s name.

Teacher key: Cyber, social, and verbal can overlap.

Cut card · Lesson 2

Backpack

A larger student repeatedly grabs a smaller student’s backpack and dumps it out while others laugh.

Teacher key: Physical and social harm; the size and audience may add power.

Cut card · Lesson 2

Rumor chain

A rumor is whispered at school and then repeated in a group chat.

Teacher key: Verbal, social, and cyber.

Message/pledge: The four kinds can overlap. Name the actions, then report the facts.
Student commitment: I will notice hidden social and online harm—not only physical harm.
Lesson 3 · Upstander, Not Bystander · Grades 3–5

The 3S upstander plan

01

Speak or redirect

If safe: “That’s not okay.” Change the subject, interrupt the audience, or invite the person away.

02

Support

Check in privately, sit beside the person, include them, and avoid blaming.

03

Send for help

Report to a trusted adult. For danger, get help immediately instead of stepping in.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 3

Recess exclusion

A group repeatedly blocks one student from joining and laughs when the student asks why.

Teacher key: Any safe combination can work: invite the student, name the exclusion, and report the pattern.

Cut card · Lesson 3

Threat

A student threatens to hit someone after school.

Teacher key: Send for adult help immediately; do not confront alone.

Cut card · Lesson 3

Group chat

A humiliating meme about a classmate is being passed around.

Teacher key: Do not react or forward. Preserve evidence privately if safe, support the classmate, and report.

Message/pledge: Upstanders do not need to be heroes. They choose the safest helpful next step.
Student commitment: Today I will interrupt safely, support someone, or send for help.
Lesson 4 · Speak Up · Grades 3–5

Report the facts

01

Start

“I need to report possible bullying.”

02

Who + What

Name people involved and the specific actions—not character labels.

03

When + Where + Pattern

Say where, when, how often, and whether it may happen again.

04

Safety + Evidence

Say what is needed now. Share evidence privately without forwarding it.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 4

Bus line

For three days, older students block a younger student from getting on the bus line and threaten to embarrass them online.

Teacher key: A strong report includes all four and asks for help before dismissal.

Cut card · Lesson 4

Group chat

A rumor is repeatedly posted about a classmate. You have a screenshot.

Teacher key: Show the evidence privately to a trusted adult. Do not keep the rumor moving.

Cut card · Lesson 4

No response yet

You reported, but the behavior continues.

Teacher key: Report again, name that it continues, and tell another trusted adult or administrator.

Message/pledge: Tattling tries to create trouble. Reporting gives facts to get safety and help.
Student commitment: I will report who, what, when, where, the pattern, and what safety help is needed.
Lesson 5 · Make It Right · Grades 3–5

Accountable repair

01

Name the action

Say exactly what you did without “if,” “but,” or blaming.

02

Name the impact

Show you understand what became unsafe or disrespectful.

03

Offer repair

Ask what can be repaired; restore property, truth, inclusion, or support.

04

Change the pattern

Accept consequences and show different behavior over time.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 5

Deflecting apology

“I was only joking, but sorry if you were offended.”

Teacher key: Revision: “I repeated a hurtful joke after you asked me to stop. I embarrassed you. I will stop and correct the rumor with the people who heard it.”

Cut card · Lesson 5

Boundary

The student who was harmed says, “I do not want to talk with you right now.”

Teacher key: Respect the boundary. Adults can plan repair without forced contact.

Cut card · Lesson 5

Pledge evidence

“We will be kind” is hard to measure. Make it observable.

Teacher key: Use specific actions: “We include, report harm, and use names and identities respectfully.”

Message/pledge: Our pledge: We notice harm, include others, report clearly, respect boundaries, and repair with changed actions.
Student commitment: I will create Cypher evidence by ___.
