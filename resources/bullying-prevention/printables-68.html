Lesson 1 · Name It · Grades 6–8

The three-test lens

01

Power

A real or perceived advantage: status, identity, numbers, strength, access, information, or technology.

02

Pattern

Repeated—or likely to repeat because posts, shares, threats, or group dynamics keep the harm active.

03

Harm

Unwanted aggression that intimidates, humiliates, isolates, controls, or causes physical or emotional harm.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 1

“Just jokes”

A student with social influence targets the same classmate’s accent each week. Others laugh. The classmate has asked them to stop.

Teacher key: Possible bullying—social power, pattern, unwanted harm, and identity-based targeting are present.

Cut card · Lesson 1

Private post, public impact

An embarrassing image is shared once in a group chat, then repeatedly screenshotted and reposted by others.

Teacher key: Possible cyberbullying—the repeated circulation can keep the harm active. Save evidence privately; do not reshare.

Cut card · Lesson 1

Argument

Two students with similar social power trade insults during one argument and both later stop.

Teacher key: Likely conflict or misconduct, not enough evidence of a power imbalance or pattern. It still needs repair and may need an adult response.

Message/pledge: Impact and evidence matter more than “I was joking.” You do not need to prove the case before reporting it.
Student commitment: I will report facts about power, pattern, and harm and let adults investigate.
Lesson 2 · Know It · Grades 6–8

Four forms can stack

01

Physical

Force, restraint, threats of force, property damage, or repeated physical intimidation.

02

Verbal

Threats, humiliation, slurs, degrading comments, or repeated targeted “jokes.”

03

Relational

Exclusion, rumor campaigns, status manipulation, coercion, or group isolation.

04

Cyber

Posts, DMs, images, group chats, gaming, impersonation, or repeated sharing that extends harm.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 2

Dogpile

One student posts a humiliating clip. Several others add insults and tag more people to join.

Teacher key: All three. Reposting and tagging can extend the pattern. Save evidence; do not amplify it.

Cut card · Lesson 2

Access as power

A team captain threatens to exclude a classmate from practice unless the classmate shares homework answers.

Teacher key: Verbal and relational; role-based access creates a power imbalance.

Cut card · Lesson 2

Hallway + group chat

A student is shoved at school, then mocked in the class group chat for reacting.

Teacher key: All four may be present.

Message/pledge: Visible and hidden harm both count. Save evidence privately; never repost it to prove a point.
Student commitment: I will name the behavior, protect the evidence, and report without amplifying the harm.
Lesson 3 · Upstander, Not Bystander · Grades 6–8

Five options—choose for safety

01

Interrupt

Name the behavior only if it is safe.

02

Redirect

Change the audience, topic, or location.

03

Support

Check in privately and restore connection.

04

Document

Preserve evidence privately; never repost or solicit more harm.

05

Report

Give facts to an adult and escalate urgent threats immediately.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 3

Cafeteria audience

A socially influential student humiliates a classmate while a crowd watches and records.

Teacher key: Reduce the audience, support the targeted student, and get adult help. Do not add another recording.

Cut card · Lesson 3

Anonymous account

An account posts identity-based insults about students and asks followers to submit names.

Teacher key: Preserve URLs/screenshots privately and report. Do not amplify or try to expose the operator yourself.

Cut card · Lesson 3

Immediate danger

A student says they are going to attack a classmate after dismissal.

Teacher key: Report immediately to an adult. Support the target only if doing so does not delay the urgent report.

Message/pledge: Effective upstanders reduce the audience, increase support, preserve evidence, and bring in help.
Student commitment: I will choose a move that is both safe and helpful—even when no one applauds it.
Lesson 4 · Speak Up · Grades 6–8

The Matchbook report frame

01

Open

“I need to report possible bullying or harassment.”

02

Facts

Who, what actions, when, where, witnesses—separate what you saw from what you heard.

03

Pattern + Evidence

Frequency, prior reports, screenshots/links shown privately, and risk of repetition.

04

Safety now

Immediate danger, retaliation concerns, and what support is needed today.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 4

Identity-based targeting

A student receives repeated slurs in school and in DMs. Peers say it is “just their humor.”

Teacher key: Include all four. Identity-based harassment may require additional school civil-rights procedures.

Cut card · Lesson 4

Secondhand information

A friend tells you about threats you did not personally see.

Teacher key: Clearly distinguish secondhand information and report it; do not crowdsource proof.

Cut card · Lesson 4

Continued harm

A report was made, but the online targeting continues and students fear retaliation.

Teacher key: Update an adult promptly with new evidence and explicit retaliation concerns.

Message/pledge: A strong report is specific, private, and clear about safety. Reporting is action—not gossip.
Student commitment: I will report facts, pattern, evidence, and immediate safety needs to an adult who can act.
Lesson 5 · Make It Right · Grades 6–8

The accountability test

01

Specific ownership

Names the action without excuses, minimizing, or demanding agreement.

02

Impact + boundary

Acknowledges harm and accepts the other person’s need for space or support.

03

Repair + consequence

Offers appropriate repair and does not treat repair as a way to avoid consequences.

04

Changed pattern

Shows different behavior over time and stops retaliation.

Scenario cards

Cut card · Lesson 5

Public apology, private retaliation

A student apologizes in front of adults, then tells friends to freeze out the reporting student.

Teacher key: Image-management and retaliation. The harmful pattern continues.

Cut card · Lesson 5

No forced reconciliation

The targeted student does not want a face-to-face restorative conversation.

Teacher key: Respect the boundary. Accountability, consequences, and other forms of repair can continue without forced contact.

Cut card · Lesson 5

Pledge test

“No drama” could silence reporting. Revise it.

Teacher key: Use protective language: “We report harm privately to adults who can act, and we do not amplify it.”

Message/pledge: Our pledge: We do not normalize harm. We report without amplifying, support without controlling, respect boundaries, and prove repair through changed behavior.
Student commitment: I will produce one observable piece of Cypher evidence today.
