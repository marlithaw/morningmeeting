Full supplied curriculumContent coverage

1. Do not ask, “Who has had this happen?” “Who has done this?” or anything else that solicits public disclosure.

2. Do not use names or identifying details from real school incidents.

3. Students never physically reenact pantsing, grabbing, clothing-pulling, blocking, restraint, or any other boundary violation.

4. Practice focuses on language, decision-making, moving away, getting help, and checking in.

5. Nervous laughter does not automatically mean someone thinks an incident is funny. Do not ask students to infer consent from facial expression.

6. A student who experienced harm is not responsible for comforting the person who caused it or participating in a face-to-face repair.

7. If a disclosure occurs, acknowledge it briefly and calmly, protect the student's privacy, and follow established school reporting/support procedures outside the public lesson.

8. Avoid blanket legal labels. The universal instructional point is precise enough: deliberately pulling clothing, exposing someone's body, or unwanted touching is a serious bodily-boundary and dignity violation requiring adult response.
