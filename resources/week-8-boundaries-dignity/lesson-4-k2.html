Slide 1 · Welcome · 0:00

Script: “This week we learned how to protect our own boundaries, recognize when a line is crossed, and act when we see harm. Today we answer the final question: What happens when I am the person who crossed the line?”

“Making a harmful choice does not have to become your identity. Accountability means dealing truthfully with the choice and changing what happens next.”

Access: Read aloud. Accept pointing, gesture, drawing, dictation, a partner response, quiet thinking, or a pass. Never require a personal story.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

Target: Tell the truth about a harmful choice and name an action that changes next time.

Slide 2 · Regulate · 2:00

Script: “Repair is difficult when our brains are busy defending ourselves. Stop. Get enough control of your body to listen. Then tell the truth.”

Do not require breathing. Offer posture change, feet on floor, quiet pause, water, or another established regulation strategy.

Access: Read aloud. Accept pointing, gesture, drawing, dictation, a partner response, quiet thinking, or a pass. Never require a personal story.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

Prepare: Display STOP → TELL THE TRUTH → FIX WHAT YOU CAN → DO BETTER. Use only the fictional shirt-pulling example.

Slide 3 · Orient · 2:00

Script: “Real repair has four parts.”

STOP — “The harmful action ends.” OWN — “Name what you did without adding ‘but.’” REPAIR — “Follow the appropriate plan to address what can be addressed.” REBUILD — “Future behavior becomes evidence.”

Clarification: Repair is not always a conversation with the harmed person. The adult-supported repair may be a written reflection, restitution, changed routine, boundary plan, restorative conference when appropriate, or another action.

Access: Read aloud. Accept pointing, gesture, drawing, dictation, a partner response, quiet thinking, or a pass. Never require a personal story.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

Model: “I pulled your shirt after you told me to stop. That was not okay. I will keep my hands in my space.” Point to the truth and then to the change.

Slide 4 · Connect · Look · 2:00

Script: “This is not a poster we hang because the words sound good. Each line describes something people should be able to experience here.”

Read each line.

After each, ask:

“What would we SEE if this were true?”

Examples:

- hands remaining off another person's clothing;

- phones staying away from someone's embarrassment;

- students getting adults rather than gathering an audience;

- changed behavior after correction.

Access: Read aloud. Accept pointing, gesture, drawing, dictation, a partner response, quiet thinking, or a pass. Never require a personal story.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

Worked response: “I was just playing” explains an intention but does not own the action. Naming the shirt-pulling tells the truth. Keeping hands in one’s own space names a change. Follow the adult-supported repair plan too.

If a student is unsure: “I ____. That was not okay. Next time I will ____.” Choose ASK FIRST / STOP / GIVE SPACE to finish.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

Slide 6 · Practice · 3:00

Script: “An excuse tries to reduce responsibility. Accountability accurately names responsibility.”

Write or display:

I did ____. It affected ____. I will repair by ____. Next time I will ____.

K–2 abbreviated frame:

I ____. That was not okay. Next time I will ____.

Important: Students do not write apologies to actual peers during this universal lesson.

6–8 challenge: Remove these four accountability-killers:

- “but”

- “everybody”

- “they made me”

- “it wasn't that serious”

The goal is not perfect wording. The goal is accurate ownership.

Access: Read aloud. Accept pointing, gesture, drawing, dictation, a partner response, quiet thinking, or a pass. Never require a personal story.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

Three-minute facilitation: 0:00–1:00: read the excuse and repair response. 1:00–2:00: students point to the truth-telling words and the next-time action. 2:00–3:00: complete the short accountability frame orally or with an adult scribe.

Look for: The response names an action and a different next choice; “sorry” alone is not treated as the entire repair.

If the response misses the skill: If a child says “They have to forgive me,” say: “They get to choose their response. Your job is to change your action.”

Slide 7 · Commit · 3:00

Script: “Choose one covenant line that you want your behavior to prove today.”

Students may keep the commitment private.

If using a physical class covenant, students may initial it later during the day. Do not spend the full instructional block forming a line to sign a poster.

Access: Read aloud. Accept pointing, gesture, drawing, dictation, a partner response, quiet thinking, or a pass. Never require a personal story.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Grades K–2 · Ready-to-use support

If support is needed: If a child says “They have to forgive me,” say: “They get to choose their response. Your job is to change your action.”

Commitment support: Offer one action from the scenario; ask the student to say, draw, dictate, or write what they will do. Do not require sharing.

Extend when ready: Match a covenant line to one action we can see today.
