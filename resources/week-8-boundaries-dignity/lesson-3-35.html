Slide 1 · Welcome · 0:00

Script: “The person causing harm is responsible for that choice. Bystanders are responsible for what they choose next. Today we are learning choices that can make a harmful moment smaller instead of bigger.”

This distinction is important: students should never leave believing a harmed student was unsafe because peers “failed” to rescue them.

Access: Offer spoken and written frames. Give quiet thinking time. Students may use fictional examples or pass. Ask for an observable detail.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 2 · Regulate · 2:00

Script: “When something happens fast, our job is not to become another unsafe person. Pause long enough to choose a move that helps.”

Say explicitly: “Helping does not mean grabbing, threatening, fighting, or putting yourself in danger.”

Access: Offer spoken and written frames. Give quiet thinking time. Students may use fictional examples or pass. Ask for an observable detail.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 3 · Orient · 2:00

Teacher modeling:

Direct: “Stop. Hands off.” Not: a speech, insult, threat, or argument.

Distract: “Bell's ringing. Clear the hallway.” The goal is to break momentum or audience attention.

Delegate: “I need an adult at the west restroom now. Someone is crossing a body boundary.” Specific information gets faster help.

Delay: “Are you okay? Do you want me to walk with you to an adult?” Do not demand details.

K–2: Teach the plain-language labels first; expose students to the D-word underneath but do not require memorization.

Safety: Direct is optional and only when safe. Get adult help promptly for ongoing harm. Delay means checking in afterward, not waiting to report danger.

Access: Offer spoken and written frames. Give quiet thinking time. Students may use fictional examples or pass. Ask for an observable detail.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 4 · Connect · Look · 2:00

Script: “Look at actions, not facial expressions. Which students are feeding the crowd? Which students are reducing the audience, getting help, or supporting the person?”

6–8 extension: “A crowd can create a social reward—attention, laughter, video, status. Removing that reward can be an intervention.”

Access: Offer spoken and written frames. Give quiet thinking time. Students may use fictional examples or pass. Ask for an observable detail.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 5 · Connect · Share · 1:00

Script: “Not every student should use Direct every time. Safety matters. Sometimes Delegate is the smartest and strongest move.”

Misconception correction: Getting adult help for a bodily-safety concern is not “tattling.” Tattling seeks to get someone in trouble over a minor issue; safety reporting seeks protection or help.

Access: Offer spoken and written frames. Give quiet thinking time. Students may use fictional examples or pass. Ask for an observable detail.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 6 · Practice · 3:00

Critical direction: “No one acts out the boundary violation. One person reads the scenario. The other person rehearses only the intervention.”

K–2: Teacher points to the four visual cards and allows students to select rather than generate language independently.

3–5: Require students to explain why the D fits.

6–8: Require sequence thinking: “What would you do first? What would you do afterward?”

Look for: Safe, brief, behavior-focused language.

Do not accept: “You're disgusting.” “What's wrong with you?” “I'll beat you up.” “Everybody record this.”

Redirect to: “Stop. Hands off.” “Give them space.” “I need an adult here.”

Safety: Direct is optional and only when safe. Get adult help promptly for ongoing harm. Delay means checking in afterward, not waiting to report danger.

Access: Offer spoken and written frames. Give quiet thinking time. Students may use fictional examples or pass. Ask for an observable detail.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.
