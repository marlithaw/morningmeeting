Slide 1 · Welcome · 0:00

Script: “Today we're talking about something every person in this room has: a body and the right to make choices about it. Our question is: What does it look like to respect another person's body and space? You will not be asked to tell personal stories. We are going to learn a skill and practice it.”

Look for: Students understand that the lesson concerns everyday respect, not punishment or investigation.

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 2 · Regulate · 2:00

Script: “Before we talk about boundaries, notice your own space. You do not need to close your eyes or take a deep breath. Feel what is supporting your body. Notice where your hands, feet, chair, or body are in relation to other people. Make any small adjustment that helps you feel ready while keeping everyone's space protected.”

Facilitation: Do not ask children to stretch arms into “personal bubbles.” The concept can be learned without bringing bodies closer together.

Look for: Students settle while maintaining physical boundaries.

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 3 · Orient · 2:00

K–2 script: “Your body belongs to you. That means people ask before touching. If someone asks you for a hug, high five, or other touch, you can say yes, no, or not now. When we hear someone's answer, we respect it.”

3–5 script: “Bodily autonomy means each person has the right to make decisions about their own body. Your clothing is also part of your physical boundary. Friendship does not remove the need to respect someone's space.”

6–8 script: “Autonomy is control over your own body. Consent is clear permission. We do not turn silence, embarrassment, friendship, nervous laughter, or what happened last time into permission for this time.”

Check: “If someone said yes yesterday, what do we know about today?” Expected: We still need to respect today's choice.

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 4 · Connect · Look · 2:00

Script: “Don't decide whether each person is ‘nice’ or ‘mean.’ Look only at the evidence. Who asked? Who answered? Who respected the answer?”

Facilitation: Give 20–30 seconds of silent noticing before soliciting responses.

Look for: Students use observable evidence rather than “they probably didn't care.”

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 5 · Connect · Share · 1:00

Look for: Students connect respect to behaviors such as asking, listening, stopping, giving room, or accepting an answer.

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.

Slide 6 · Practice · 3:00

Script: “Our skill is not complicated: See it. Name it. Do it. First notice the situation. Then name whose boundary or choice is involved. Then choose the respectful action.”

K–2 facilitation: Teacher models both “yes” and “no.” Students practice responding “Okay” without pouting, bargaining, or demanding a reason.

3–5 facilitation: Emphasize that touching a pencil and putting an arm around a person are not the same type of decision.

6–8 facilitation: Challenge “We're friends” and “we always do this” without shaming. Ask: “Who is making the decision in that sentence?”

Look for: The student's alternative returns the choice to the other person.

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.

Practice boundary: Fictional situations only. No personal disclosures or physical reenactments.

Slide 7 · Commit · 3:00

Script: “Choose something specific enough that another person could see it. ‘Be respectful’ is a goal. ‘Ask before touching someone's things or body’ is an action.”

Look for: Observable commitments.

Access: Allow private writing or a pass. Use fictional scenarios. Support conclusions with observable evidence, not assumptions about feelings.
