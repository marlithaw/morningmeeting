My Body. My Boundary.

I can: Ask, wait for an answer, and accept yes, no, or not now.

You want to give a friend a hug.

SEE IT: You want to hug them.

NAME IT: Their body = their choice.

DO IT: “Can I give you a hug?”

Partner answers Yes / No / Not now.

Your response every time: “Okay.”

Words and decisions only. No touching or reenactment. You may pass.

Monday · Grades K–2 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

Give each pair YES, NO, and NOT NOW cards. Use the Ask → Wait → Respect strip. No partner touching.

Say and model

Teacher voices both roles: “Can I give you a hug?” “Not now.” “Okay.” Keep hands resting throughout.

Three-minute practice

0:00–1:00: model yes and no. 1:00–2:00: one child reads or points to ASK; the partner chooses an answer card; the asker says “Okay.” 2:00–3:00: switch roles and repeat.

Worked answer

Maya asks Leo for a high five and Leo says yes: Leo chooses. Eli reaches for Nia’s hat without asking: Nia has not been given a choice. Zoe accepts Ana’s no: Ana’s choice is respected.

Response scaffold

Point to ASK, WAIT, or RESPECT. Choose YES / NO / NOT NOW. An adult can voice the chosen response.

Look for

The asker pauses for an answer and accepts it without bargaining, touching, or asking for a reason.

If the response misses the skill

If a child reaches toward a partner, pause: “We practice words only. Hands rest. Ask, then wait.” Model again with cards.

Extend when ready

Change the answer from yes to not now. Does your respectful response change? It remains “Okay.”

Expected exit response

Before touching someone, I ask and wait. When they answer, I respect it.

Tuesday · Grades K–2 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

Display three check cards: ASKED? KIND? SAFE? Read Jordan and Sam’s fictional shirt-pulling scenario; do not act it out.

Say and model

“Sam said stop. Jordan is still pulling. The pulling must stop. We do not need to guess whether Jordan meant to be funny.”

Three-minute practice

0:00–1:00: read the scenario and point to each check. 1:00–2:00: children choose STOP or KEEP GOING cards, then identify one check. 2:00–3:00: rehearse “Stop. Hands off” or “I need a grown-up.”

Worked answer

A: both agreed to the handshake. B: hiding the pencil needs a respectful return and repair, even if it is returned quickly. C: clothing-grabbing with a stop request must end immediately. Jordan must release Sam’s shirt and give space; an adult can help.

Response scaffold

Point to STOP / KEEP GOING. Complete “Jordan needs to ____.” Use an adult-read scenario and a drawn stop sign if speaking is hard.

Look for

The student names the action or stop words, chooses to stop, and does not rely on someone smiling or laughing.

If the response misses the skill

If a child says “Sam looked happy,” say: “We do not guess from a face. Sam said stop. What needs to happen?”

Extend when ready

What if Sam freezes and says nothing? No clear permission is still not permission.

Expected exit response

Asked? Kind? Safe? If someone says stop, the action stops.

Wednesday · Grades K–2 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

Lay out SAY IT, SHIFT IT, GET HELP, and CHECK IN cards. Identify the actual trusted adult students can approach in this setting.

Say and model

Point to GET HELP: “I need a grown-up. Someone is pulling clothing at recess.” Model walking toward help without involving another child’s body.

Three-minute practice

0:00–1:00: read the recess scenario and model getting help. 1:00–2:00: each child selects a helping card and says or points to its words. 2:00–3:00: switch roles; choose an afterward check-in.

Worked answer

Moving toward an adult and asking peers to give space help. Laughing adds an audience. Later, “Are you okay?” offers care without demanding details. If it is unsafe to speak to the person, get an adult.

Response scaffold

Choose “Stop. Hands off,” “I need a grown-up,” or “Come with me.” Pointing is a complete response; the teacher can voice the words.

Look for

The child selects a safe move and can indicate whom to ask for help. No touching, arguing, or threatening.

If the response misses the skill

If a child chooses fighting, say: “Helping keeps your body safe too. Choose GET HELP and tell me the words.”

Extend when ready

Choose one move now and one kind check-in for afterward.

Expected exit response

I can get a grown-up. I can check in without asking someone to tell me what happened.

Thursday · September 24 · Grades K–2 · Student tool

Repair Is An Action

I can: Tell the truth about a harmful choice and name an action that changes next time.

Instead of: “I was just playing.”

Try: “I pulled your shirt after you told me to stop. That was not okay. I will keep my hands in my space.”

Task: Point to the part that tells the truth. Point to the part that changes next time.

Words and decisions only. No touching or reenactment. You may pass.

TELL THE TRUTH

MAKE A DIFFERENT CHOICE

Point, say, draw, or tell an adult: “I ____. That was not okay. Next time I will ____.” Choose ASK FIRST / STOP / GIVE SPACE to finish.

My commitment

I will protect bodies and privacy by ____.

Thursday · Grades K–2 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

Display STOP → TELL THE TRUTH → FIX WHAT YOU CAN → DO BETTER. Use only the fictional shirt-pulling example.

Say and model

“I pulled your shirt after you told me to stop. That was not okay. I will keep my hands in my space.” Point to the truth and then to the change.

Three-minute practice

0:00–1:00: read the excuse and repair response. 1:00–2:00: students point to the truth-telling words and the next-time action. 2:00–3:00: complete the short accountability frame orally or with an adult scribe.

Worked answer

“I was just playing” explains an intention but does not own the action. Naming the shirt-pulling tells the truth. Keeping hands in one’s own space names a change. Follow the adult-supported repair plan too.

Response scaffold

“I ____. That was not okay. Next time I will ____.” Choose ASK FIRST / STOP / GIVE SPACE to finish.

Look for

The response names an action and a different next choice; “sorry” alone is not treated as the entire repair.

If the response misses the skill

If a child says “They have to forgive me,” say: “They get to choose their response. Your job is to change your action.”

Extend when ready

Match a covenant line to one action we can see today.

Expected exit response

Making things right means telling the truth, following the repair plan, and making a different choice.
