Teach Respect and Responsibility

Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday, each grade band follows the day’s focus, key message, YouTube clip, think-and-talk prompt, toolkit worksheet, and take-home card. There is no school for students on Tuesday, November 3. Friday’s Morning Spark (Friday Wonder) carries the same theme, followed by Friday Cypher.

Timing: Welcome ready screen; Regulate 2, Orient 2, Connect 3 (Look 2 and Share 1), Practice 3, Commit 3, Transition 2 minutes.

Respect and privacy, every day: use the fictional situations on the slides. Students talk about their own choices or the slide’s story, never a classmate’s mistakes or behavior. Self-ratings and commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Welcome responses by writing, drawing, speaking, pointing, signing, or a student’s usual communication tool.

Video clips: each “Watch 60 seconds” button opens the YouTube video with captions on and stops at one minute. If a video will not load, read the key message aloud a second time and continue.

Participation moves for every lesson

Equity sticks (cold call): keep one stick per student in a “not yet called” cup. Always give think time first, then say, “Everyone has an answer. I will pull a stick.” Pull, read the name, ask the question, and move the stick to the “called” cup. If a student needs a moment, say “I will come back to you,” call one more student, and return. Accept pointing, signing, or a device response. The scripts name exactly when to pull a stick.

Turn and talk: partners A and B. “Partner A answers first. Partner B, listen so you can repeat it.” After 30 seconds: “Switch.” Then cold call: “Tell us what your partner said.” K–2 partners sit knee to knee for 10 to 15 seconds.

Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “Do you agree or disagree? Why?” “Say it in your own words.” “What in the video or slide makes you think that?”

LiveSchool points: award during the work, name the Safe, Respectful, or Responsible behavior out loud, then tap. Each lesson script includes the exact words. This week is LiveSchool Store week, so connect points to the choices students practice in every space. Never award points for being quiet or for finishing first, and never take points for Level 1 behavior during these lessons.

Behavior supports: keep the lesson moving and correct privately. Use proximity, a quiet name, and a direct reminder of the expectation. Each day’s Transition note has the exact words for the most likely disruption. Follow up after the meeting through the restorative process. Any report of teasing, bullying, or recording a classmate is handled under campus reporting procedures the same day.

Monday November 2 · Grades K–2 · Indiana SEL 5A and 5B · Connection

Respect Is Something You Do

Target: I can show respect with my voice, my body, and my words.

Key message: Respect means treating people the way they deserve to be treated. We show it with a calm voice, a safe body, and kind words.

Prepare: Respect Detective page from the K–2 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: How to Show Respect (Sesame Street).

Evidence: The child points to or names a respectful choice and shows one respectful action with voice, body, or words.

Differentiate: Kindergarten: point to the pictures and circle with adult support. Grade 1: say the respectful words aloud. Grade 2: write one respectful sentence. Drawing, pointing, and dictating all count.

Answer notes: Listening and gentle hands are respectful choices. Grabbing a toy is not. Any drawing that shows a calm voice, a safe body, or kind words is correct.

Take home: Respect at Home. Family chat: How do we show respect at home?

Respect Is Something You Do

Target: I can show respect through my actions, even when I disagree.

Key message: Respect is an action. It shows in how we listen, how we disagree, and how we treat other people’s space and things.

Prepare: Looks Like, Sounds Like page from the 3–5 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Give It, Live It, RESPECT (Sesame Street).

Evidence: The student names respectful actions under Looks Like and Sounds Like and writes a disagreement that targets the idea, not the person.

Differentiate: Grade 3: use the sentence starters on the Practice slide. Grades 4–5: explain why the starter keeps the conversation respectful. Students may draw and dictate.

Answer notes: Looks like: eyes on the speaker, calm body, waiting for a turn, hands off other people’s things. Sounds like: calm voice, “I see it differently because…”, “Can you explain?” A respectful disagreement names the idea, not the person.

Respect Is Something You Do

Target: I can show respect in how I listen, respond, and disagree, especially when it is hard.

Key message: Respect does not depend on liking someone. It is how we treat every person, including people we disagree with. Tone, body language, and words all send a message.

Prepare: Respond, Don’t React page from the 6–8 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Amazing Kids of Character: Respect (DCMP).

Evidence: The rewrite keeps the student’s own position, removes the put-down, and names one tone cue and one body cue.

Differentiate: Grade 6: use the starters from the slide. Grades 7–8: explain how the same words can be respectful or disrespectful depending on tone. Accept oral responses.

Answer notes: “Whatever” becomes “I am not sure I agree. Can you say more?” “That makes no sense” becomes “Help me understand your point.” An eye roll becomes facing the speaker and waiting to respond. A respectful tone is calm and steady.

Take home: Respect Under Pressure. Family chat: When is it hardest to stay respectful, and what helps?

Prepare: Fix-It Steps page from the K–2 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Elmo Didn’t Mean To (Sesame Street).

Own It, Fix It

Target: I can take responsibility for my impact, even when I did not mean it, and repair it.

Key message: What we meant and what happened are not always the same. Responsibility means owning the impact, not just explaining the intent, and then repairing it.

Prepare: Intent, Impact, Repair page from the 6–8 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Amazing Kids of Character: Courage (DCMP).

Evidence: The student sorts all four responses correctly and writes a repair plan that names the impact and one concrete action.

Differentiate: Grade 6: use the sorted ownership lines as models. Grades 7–8: explain why “I’m sorry, but…” cancels an apology. Accept oral responses.

Answer notes: Excuses: “They’re too sensitive.” “Everybody else was doing it.” Ownership: “I didn’t think about how that would land. I’m sorry.” “I’ll delete it and talk to them.” A strong repair names the impact, apologizes without “but,” and includes an action.

Take home: Repair Conversation. Family chat: What does a real repair look like in our family?
