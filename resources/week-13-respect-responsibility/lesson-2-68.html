Matchbook Morning Meeting · Own It, Fix It · Respect and ResponsibilityGrades 6–8 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. Everyone makes mistakes. Responsible people own them and fix them. Today’s focus is Own It, Fix It.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for today: we only talk about our own choices or the story on the slide. No one shares a classmate’s mistake.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes. Then read the third card aloud: “Calm brain, clear choices.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can take responsibility for my impact, even when I did not mean it, and repair it.” Point to each picture and read its label. Ask students to read the goal with you. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Camila, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: Why does owning a mistake take courage?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Amazing Kids of Character: Courage, DCMP). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Jordan, why does owning a mistake take courage?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “A joke you posted in the group chat hurt someone. You say, “I was only joking.”” Then read each question: Why is “only joking” not enough? What would a real repair include? Give ten seconds of silent think time. Turn and talk: “Partner A answers the first question. Partner B answers the second. Go.” After 30 seconds, say “Switch.” Equity stick: pull two sticks. Ask Brianna, “What did your partner say?” Ask Isaiah, “Do you agree or want to add on? Say more.” Close with the slide line: “Intent explains. Impact matters. Repair rebuilds trust.” Strong response: “Only joking” explains intent but ignores impact. A real repair names the impact, apologizes without “but,” and includes an action: delete the post, check in privately, and change what gets posted next time.

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Intent, Impact, Repair page from the 6–8 toolkit. Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step in one sentence, then release. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Responsible: “Xavier, you sorted ‘I’m sorry, but…’ as an excuse and explained why. That is owning impact.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The student sorts all four responses correctly and writes a repair plan that names the impact and one concrete action. Access: Grade 6: use the sorted ownership lines as models. Grades 7–8: explain why “I’m sorry, but…” cancels an apology. Accept oral responses.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Repair Conversation. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “When my impact does not match my intent, I will stop explaining and say, “I see it hurt. I’m sorry.” One repair action I can take is deleting the post and checking in privately.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Own your impact today: in class, in the hallway, and online.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If the group chat scenario turns into talk about a real incident, redirect: “We are using the slide’s example only. If something real is going on, see me after.” Follow up privately and use campus reporting procedures if needed.
