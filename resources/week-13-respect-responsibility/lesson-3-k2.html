Matchbook Morning Meeting · Respect and Responsibility Everywhere · Respect and ResponsibilityGrades K–2 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. Respect and responsibility go with us everywhere: the hallway, lunch, recess, and every class. Today’s focus is Respect and Responsibility Everywhere.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for today: we use the situations on the slides. If something real is going on, students can talk with an adult after the meeting.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes. Then read the third card aloud: “Ready for every space.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can be safe, respectful, and responsible in every place at school.” Point to each picture and read its label. Have students repeat each label with a hand motion. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Keyla, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: How do the friends show respect while they play?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Respect Is a Home Run!, Sesame Street). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Darius, how do the friends show respect while they play?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “You drop your trash at lunch. No one sees.” Point to choice A, then choice B, and read each one. Say: “Think. Do not call out. Point when I say go.” Give ten seconds of think time, then say “Go.” Students point to the slide or hold up a Show-Me card. Equity stick: pull a stick and ask, “Nia, which choice did you point to?” Then pull a second stick: “Tell us why.” Accept pointing, signing, or a whispered answer to a partner. Answer: choice A, “I pick it up.” Name it: “That choice is responsible, even when no one sees.” Talk move: “Who can say it in their own words?”

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Every Place Match page from the K–2 toolkit. Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step on a copy under the document camera, or hold up the page and point. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Responsible: “Mateo, you pushed in your chair without a reminder. That is responsible in every place.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The child matches each place to a good choice and names one place to practice today. Access: Kindergarten: point to each place, then draw a line with adult support. Grade 1: say the choice for each place. Grade 2: write one sentence about the place they chose.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Good Choices Everywhere. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “At recess today, I will take turns on the slide.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Show respect and responsibility in the hallway, at lunch, and at recess.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If a student says “nobody will know,” respond calmly: “You will know. Show me the responsible choice.” Praise the choice as soon as you see it.
