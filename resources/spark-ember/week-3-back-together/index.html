Week 3 · Teacher delivery guide

October 19–23, 2026. Morning Meeting is Monday–Thursday; Spark and Ember include Friday. Use the school’s established Friday Cypher plan. No 2026–27 closure calendar was supplied; adjust a dated board if your school calendar differs.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45. IXL is pinned to Week 8 from the existing dated source.

Use day keys 1–5, arrows, bilingual read aloud, and whole-week preview. Wednesday reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Clips are followed by a quiet pause; students may observe. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Monday privacy and follow-up

Keep the reference format for each band. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. Do not collect public votes or require an explanation. Students fold the slip and place it in the box. Review adult-check-in requests privately and follow up through the school’s existing process. The reveal is a fresh readiness practice, not a “correct answer” to someone’s check-in.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday October 19

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in.

Reveal / key: Put your feet comfortably on the floor. Relax your hands. Find one familiar thing in our room. You may point instead of talking.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior.

Ember video bridge: Watch Rosita practice belly breathing. Stay seated; breathe comfortably or observe. Then notice one action from your own day.

Reflection: Draw one person you greeted today. Add a wave or your greeting. I said hello to ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Tuesday October 20

Spark task: Look at the desk. Count the pencils. Which two things help you draw and write? Point or draw.

Reveal / key: There are three pencils. A pencil and paper help us draw and write. The ball belongs away from the writing space.

Differentiate / facilitate: K: touch-count three objects. Grade 1: explain the function of pencil and paper. Accept pointing.

Ember video bridge: Watch the breathing model. Let your breath stay comfortable; you may simply observe. Then return to the reflection.

Reflection: Draw one direction you followed. Show the part you checked. I checked ____. Then I ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Wednesday October 21

Spark task: What might this be? Draw a guess. Point to one color or shape clue.

Reveal / key: Crayon tips! We can see colored points and paper wrappers. Which clue helped you?

Differentiate / facilitate: Accept a reasonable guess with a visible clue, even when the guess changes.

Ember video bridge: The sky is a metaphor for thoughts and feelings that change. You do not need to make a feeling disappear before reflecting.

Reflection: Draw a time you needed help, or a time you could ask tomorrow. Please help me with ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Thursday October 22

Spark task: Would you rather draw a garden or build a garden with blocks? Choose one. Show one thing you would add.

Reveal / key: Both choices can show a garden. Name or point to a plant in your plan. Tell your partner why you chose this way.

Differentiate / facilitate: K: point and draw one plant. Grade 1: give a reason and label the drawing.

Ember video bridge: Notice the message of self-acceptance. Take a quiet pause afterward. Reflect on your own actions without comparing people.

Reflection: Draw two people sharing a tool. Show what each person can do. First ____. Then ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Friday October 23

Spark task: Wonder: what will happen to the shadow if we move the light? Draw your prediction.

Reveal / key: Try it: keep the object still and move the flashlight to the side. Watch where the shadow goes. Compare with your prediction.

Differentiate / facilitate: Teacher demonstrates with a flashlight aimed at the object, never at eyes. Students predict before seeing the light move.

Ember video bridge: Listen for a strength word. You do not have to feel every word in the song. Take a quiet pause, then name one action you can repeat.

Reflection: Choose one helpful thing you did this week. Draw yourself doing it again. I can ____ again.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Tuesday October 20

Spark task: The library cart has three shelves. How many books are there? Show two ways to group them.

Reveal / key: There are twelve books: 4 + 4 + 4. You could also group them as 6 + 6 or six pairs. Show how your groups include every book once.

Differentiate / facilitate: Grade 2: count and write repeated addition. Grade 3: connect equal groups to multiplication. Groups must include each book once.

Ember video bridge: Watch Rosita practice belly breathing. Stay seated; breathe comfortably or observe. Then notice one action from your own day.

Reflection: Name a direction you checked today. How did checking help? I checked ____. That helped me ____.

Wednesday October 21

Differentiate / facilitate: Name texture or overlapping shapes. A revised answer with better evidence is success.

Ember video bridge: The sky is a metaphor for thoughts and feelings that change. You do not need to make a feeling disappear before reflecting.

Reflection: Choose a real or possible stuck moment. Ask for the help that would fit. I tried ____. Please help me ____.

Thursday October 22

Spark task: Would you rather solve a twelve-piece puzzle or make a four-panel story with a partner? How could both people contribute?

Reveal / key: Puzzle: each could start with six pieces, then connect them together. Story: each could plan two panels, then check the order together. Other useful plans are welcome.

Differentiate / facilitate: Grade 2: draw roles. Grade 3: explain how both roles contribute to one finished product.

Ember video bridge: Listen for a strength word. You do not have to feel every word in the song. Take a quiet pause, then name one action you can repeat.

Reflection: Sketch a fair turn-taking plan for a tool you share in class. While ____ uses it, ____ can ____. Then ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Friday October 23

Spark task: Wonder: can one sheet of paper hold more counters if we fold it? Draw a fair test.

Reveal / key: Compare equal paper across the same gap. Change only the folds. Add the same kind of counters one at a time. Count and compare.

Differentiate / facilitate: Use equal-size paper, identical counters, and equal book supports. Keep the gap constant. Prediction is not the answer; observation is evidence.

Ember video bridge: Notice the message of self-acceptance. Take a quiet pause afterward. Reflect on your own actions without comparing people.

Reflection: Choose your most useful classroom move this week. Where will you use it again? My move: ____. Next time: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Tuesday October 20

Spark task: You have 22 minutes. Use the task board to make a plan with 2 minutes left to check. Can all three tasks fit? Show your reasoning.

Reveal / key: Yes: 4 + 8 + 8 + 2 = 22. Put materials first and checking last. The reading and math tasks can switch order. Explain your choice.

Differentiate / facilitate: Grade 4: add durations. Grade 5: annotate start/end times and compare two orders. No need to race.

Ember video bridge: Notice how the technique labels a thought or feeling and returns attention. Try it silently if useful; then separate an event from your interpretation.

Reflection: Recall a message you checked, or write a check you wish you had used. Message: ____. My check: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Wednesday October 21

Spark task: Infer the object from two visible details. After the reveal, name the clue that best supports your answer.

Reveal / key: A bicycle’s gears, chain, and derailleur. Toothed wheels and a chain are evidence. A still photo cannot tell us how fast the bicycle is moving.

Differentiate / facilitate: Require visible evidence. Do not infer speed from a still photo.

Ember video bridge: Notice the difference between forcing a feeling and gently returning attention. Then choose one practical next step in your learning.

Reflection: Name a stopping point in your learning and a useful help request. If you got help, what did you try next? I tried ____. I needed ____. My next attempt: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Thursday October 22

Spark task: Would you rather practice in three 4-minute rounds or two 6-minute rounds? Both include a 1-minute feedback pause after every round except the last. Which fits a 14-minute block?

Reveal / key: Both fit. A: 12 minutes of practice + 2 feedback = 14. B: 12 + 1 = 13, leaving 1 minute. Choose a plan and explain when its feedback timing would help.

Differentiate / facilitate: Grade 4: calculate total time. Grade 5: explain feedback tradeoffs for a specific skill. More pauses are not automatically better.

Ember video bridge: Notice the invitation to step back from a thought. After a quiet pause, look at one classroom situation from another point of view.

Reflection: Evaluate a partner plan from today, or the morning model. Did it address both people’s needs? Need 1: ____. Need 2: ____. One improvement: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Friday October 23

Spark task: Wonder: how could we test whether a tighter rubber band makes a different sound? Name one change and two things to keep the same.

Reveal / key: Change tension using the same band and box. Keep the plucking place and pull as similar as possible. Listen and record before deciding; repeat each setting.

Differentiate / facilitate: Teacher handles rubber-band setup; keep bands away from faces. Use gentle plucking. Do not claim a result before testing.

Ember video bridge: Consider how attention to other people can shape a choice. Then evaluate a classroom action by what it made possible for someone.

Reflection: Which skill helped your learning most this week? Use one example and choose a next step. Skill + evidence: ____. Next step: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Grades 6–8 · Daily teaching key

Monday October 19

Spark task: Readiness scale 1–5; emotion vocabulary; one controllable action.

Reveal / key: Choose one action within your control for the first work block. Make it specific: “If I lose the directions, I will reread step one and ask one precise question.” Adjust it to your own need.

Differentiate / facilitate: Students may leave the emotion word blank. Grade 6 can use the if/then model; grades 7–8 specify a likely obstacle.

Ember video bridge: Notice the invitation to step back from a thought. After a quiet pause, look at one classroom situation from another point of view.

Reflection: Evaluate one welcome or restart routine by its effect. Use an observable action, not a guess about someone’s feelings. Action: ____. What it made possible: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Tuesday October 20

Spark task: Your group has $18. Every member needs a folder. Use the supply board to propose two feasible plans for four people. Explain one tradeoff.

Reveal / key: Four folders cost $8, leaving $10. Plan A: one marker pack + three glue sticks = $10. Plan B: two marker packs + one glue stick = $10. Both total $18; choose based on the task.

Differentiate / facilitate: Grade 6: total two plans. Grades 7–8: justify the allocation for a stated task and identify an alternative with savings.

Ember video bridge: Notice how the technique labels a thought or feeling and returns attention. Try it silently if useful; then separate an event from your interpretation.

Reflection: Separate a message from an assumption you made, or use today’s fictional group message. Write a better clarifying question. Stated: ____. Assumed: ____. Question: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Wednesday October 21

Spark task: Use the crop to make a claim. Name two observations and one question you cannot answer from the image alone. Revise after the reveal.

Reveal / key: A damaged solar array on Skylab, photographed in 1973 before repair. The caption supplies the history; the photograph supplies visible structural details. Separate those sources of evidence.

Differentiate / facilitate: Separate visual observation from caption knowledge. The source identifies Skylab and the date; students cannot deduce those from the crop alone.

Ember video bridge: Notice the difference between forcing a feeling and gently returning attention. Then choose one practical next step in your learning.

Reflection: Evaluate a help request you used or could use. Would the support leave you with a clear next action? Gap: ____. Requested support: ____. My next action: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Thursday October 22

Spark task: Would you rather think alone first, then compare, or discuss first, then write? Choose for a difficult group decision. State one benefit, one risk, and a safeguard.

Reveal / key: There is no single best plan. Solo thinking can preserve independent ideas; discussion can clarify confusion. A written first idea or a round where everyone contributes can reduce one voice dominating.

Differentiate / facilitate: Grade 6: benefit and risk frame. Grades 7–8: identify a safeguard and when they would change their plan.

Ember video bridge: Consider how attention to other people can shape a choice. Then evaluate a classroom action by what it made possible for someone.

Reflection: Test a group plan against two needs and the available time. Use today’s model if you prefer. Identify one revision. Needs: ____. Time: ____. Revision and reason: ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.

Friday October 23

Spark task: Wonder: how could you test whether light angle changes a small solar panel’s output? Define a measure, a control, and a result that would challenge your prediction.

Reveal / key: Measure current or voltage with the same panel, lamp, distance, and circuit. Change the panel angle; repeat readings. A result inconsistent with your predicted pattern should make you revise it. This is a plan, not a result.

Differentiate / facilitate: A design task; equipment is not required for arrival. If testing later, teacher uses a low-voltage classroom kit and records the same measurement under the same circuit conditions.

Ember video bridge: The sky is a metaphor for thoughts and feelings that change. You do not need to make a feeling disappear before reflecting.

Reflection: Choose a skill to carry into next week. Cite one example from this week and one situation where you will need it. Evidence from this week: ____. When ____, I will ____.

Look for: a concrete action or supported next step. If no real example occurred, use the fictional morning model; do not require an invented success.
