Run the matched week

Theme: Small Moves, Strong Finish. Morning Spark gives students a low-demand entry task and a reason to notice, infer, and choose. Ember asks students to name what they actually did and its effect.

7:15: greet by name at the door; point to the Do Now. Students answer independently in 3-5 minutes. K-2 read a book afterward; grades 3-8 move to the displayed IXL Math/Reading codes. In the 2-3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL.

Announcements are the hard stop, about 7:37. After announcements, run a reveal for no more than two minutes. Give 10 seconds to think; invite one or two responses. On Monday, keep feelings private and invite volunteers only. At 7:45, hand off to Morning Meeting.

Ember: open the board and place a reflection slip before 2:05. Keep the original flow: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 calm-down video or breathing fallback, 2:13 written/drawn reflection, then seated waiting. Release only on the adult announcement. Retain the existing route order and numbered Chromebook cart return.

The existing Ember board shows walkers at 2:10, car riders/aftercare and activities at 2:15, then buses K-2 at 2:17, 3-5 at 2:20, and 6-8 at 2:23. These inherited times remain subject to the campus dismissal plan; the clock display never authorizes students to leave.

Use the classroom decks Monday through Thursday, October 5-8. Thursday’s reflection gives students a chance to name a finish and a habit they will carry forward.

Daily delivery and reveal

Monday – Check-In (start ready): Distribute a folded private slip. Each band checks in differently, then picks one small move to start ready. K–1: circle a weather-inside face (sunny, cloudy, rainy, stormy, windy, foggy, rainbow) or the talk-to-a-grown-up child. 2–3: circle a weather and one ready move (breaths, materials, read the board, ask a question). 4–5: energy battery 1–5, one small move, optional adult check-in box. 6–8: ready level 1–5, a word or two, and one move within their control. Reveal teaches a new regulation move for each band: K–1 flower-and-candle breaths; 2–3 five-finger breathing (the dot traces the hand); 4–5 squeeze and release; 6–8 5-4-3-2-1 grounding plus the math of 1% (1.01³⁶⁵ ≈ 37.8; 0.99³⁶⁵ ≈ 0.03). Never display or cold call an individual check-in. Review support requests promptly and follow established support procedures.

Tuesday – Picture of the Day (care for our shared space): One illustrated classroom scene for every band: a finger pushes the first of 20 small dominoes (red, yellow, blue, green, repeated 5 times), which lead to a finale of 4 black dominoes that each grow 1.5 times taller, ending at a finish bell. The reveal plays the chain reaction; press Push again to replay. K–1: what will happen next? Count the red dominoes (5); find the bell. 2–3: notice and wonder; the pattern repeats 5 times, so 4 × 5 = 20. 4–5: explain the chain with first, next, finally (24 dominoes in all); a missing domino breaks the chain. 6–8: 2 in × 1.5⁴ ≈ 10.1 in; a falling domino can topple one about 1.5 times its size, so the 10th domino in that chain would be about 77 in. Close by connecting the chain to the room: one small move, such as a pushed-in chair, helps the next person.

Wednesday – Zoom In (try again: revise your guess): The Do Now magnifier shows a true close-up crop of the same picture the reveal uses. The reveal opens on that exact crop, holds for a moment, then slowly zooms out for about 12 seconds; the answer panel appears only after the zoom finishes. Press Zoom again to replay. Ask students whether their guess changed during the zoom. K–1: beads on threads become a dewy spider web; circle necklace, bubbles, spider web, or draw an idea. 2–3: dew drops and 12 spokes on a garden spider web; spiders build one thread at a time. 4–5: square tiles become a monarch mosaic; 60 × 40 = 2,400 tiles (rows × columns); monarchs migrate up to 3,000 miles in the fall. 6–8: dots of pure color become a pointillist sunset of 7,238 dots; optical mixing; Seurat painted A Sunday on La Grande Jatte (1884–86), about 7 × 10 feet, at the Art Institute of Chicago.

Thursday – Would You Rather (finish strong): Each band votes on its own choice with the +1 buttons; there is no single correct vote. Invite a reason and a fair retelling of another choice. K–1: the turtle or the rabbit; in the story, small steady steps finish first. 2–3: first runner or anchor on a relay team; a smooth handoff is a small move that can win the race. 4–5: 20 minutes every day or 2 hours on Saturday; 20 × 7 = 140 minutes vs. 120, and spaced practice helps memory. 6–8: $1,000,000 today or a penny that doubles for 30 days; day 10 $5.12, day 20 $5,242.88, day 28 $1,342,177.28, day 30 $5,368,709.12. Then argue the other side.

Prepare, coach, and verify

Prepare: open the grade-band Spark deck in Chrome; test English/Spanish read aloud and full screen. Keys 1-4 select a day; arrows move Do Now -> Reveal -> Handoff. Repeat aloud runs the entry prompt for staggered arrivals. Whole-week view lets teachers preview every slide.

Print: Spark pages are two sheets per band: four Monday check-in slips to cut apart, then one response page with Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday panels. Ember sheets include two matching slips per day. Set a fixed paper/pencil location and a private check-in collection spot. Review slips before students leave whenever follow-up is needed.

Clarity: give the same short entry cue: "Do Now first, then read/IXL." Access: let students draw, point, dictate, use the sentence frame, or use an appropriate lower-demand band. Regulation: offer a brief settling option and return to the task. Honor existing IEP/504 supports.

Expectation practice uses See-It / Name-It / Do-It: show the entry or seated dismissal move; name the observable action; rehearse it with a brief reset. Recognition names sustained evidence of Safe, Respectful, or Responsible using the established LiveSchool categories.
