Week 4 · Teacher delivery guide

October 26–30, 2026 is Disability Awareness Week. Morning Spark and Ember carry the same through-line as Morning Meeting: everyone belongs. Monday–Thursday Spark keeps its fixed format rotation with a belonging, communication, respectful words, or access focus. Friday’s Spark is the Disability Awareness Week lesson Kindness into Action, with the day’s worksheet, followed by the Time for Friday Cypher slide.

Spark leads straight into Morning Meeting

Each Monday–Thursday Spark ends on a hand-off slide that names that day’s Morning Meeting lesson (Everyone Belongs, Different Ways to Learn and Communicate, Respectful Words, Fairness and Access) with a button that opens your band’s Morning Meeting deck. Grades 2–3 rooms see two buttons: grade 2 uses the K–2 lesson and grade 3 uses the 3–5 lesson. Spark is the arrival warm-up on the day’s theme; the Disability Awareness worksheet itself is completed in Morning Meeting (Monday–Thursday) and in Friday’s Spark.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. The day’s IXL codes are on the slide, top right, from IXL Week 9 (October 26–30). Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45; on Friday, advance to Time for Friday Cypher and students report to the hallway once the announcement is made.

Use day keys 1–5, arrows, bilingual read aloud, Repeat aloud for late arrivals, and Week view. Tuesday’s reveal circles the four ways to communicate. Wednesday’s reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals. Friday’s reveal has a 60-second captioned video button.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Equity sticks, talk moves, and LiveSchool

Every reveal follows Show, Think (ten silent seconds), Call (one or two equity sticks), Talk (30-second turn and talk if time allows). The daily keys below give the exact cold-call question and stem for each band. Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “What did your partner say?” Award LiveSchool points during the work period for sustained effort, said out loud: “Respectful: Amara gave her partner time to finish. That is patience.” “Safe: Dante chose to tell an adult. That protects someone.” Never award points for being quiet or finishing first.

Respect and privacy

Monday check-in slips stay private; the counselor collects them by 10:00 a.m. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. All week, use the fictional examples on the slides. Never ask students to name, explain, or show their own or a family member’s disability, diagnosis, or health needs, and never invite them to identify a classmate. If a student jokes about a picture board, device, or classroom, respond in one calm sentence (“We never joke about how people communicate.”), continue, and follow up privately. Any report of teasing is handled under campus reporting procedures the same day.

When behavior gets in the way

Correct privately and briefly, with proximity and the expectation: “Do Now first, then IXL.” Offer a frame instead of an explanation to a student who has not started. Do not hold a student out of the reveal or Morning Meeting to finish the Do Now, and do not use LiveSchool deductions for Level 1 behavior.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday October 26

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in. Reveal: a welcoming wave.

Reveal / key: Everyone belongs here. Wave or say hello to someone near you. You may wave instead of talking.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; an adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior. Reveal: model a wave and a quiet hello; both count.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Show me a wave. Who can say hello in a quiet voice?” Turn-and-talk stem: “Hello, ___! (or a wave)”

Ember video bridge: Meet Julia and watch how her friends make room for her to play her way. Then take three calm breaths in your seat. (Meet Julia)

Reflection: Draw yourself welcoming a friend to play. You can play with us!

Look for: a concrete, respectful action. Reflections never ask about anyone’s disability or health; if a student has no real example, the fictional morning model is enough.

Grades 6–8 · Daily teaching key

Monday October 26

Spark task: Readiness scale 1–5; emotion vocabulary; one controllable action. Reveal: one observable way to make room.

Reveal / key: This week is Disability Awareness Week. Belonging means respect, choice, and real chances to participate. Name one way you will make room for someone today.

Differentiate / facilitate: Readiness is information, not a grade. Reveal: push for an observable action, such as inviting a quieter peer to add an idea, rather than a general “be nice.”

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What is one observable way you will make room for someone today?” Turn-and-talk stem: “Today I will make room by ___ during ___.”

IXL codes on the slide: Grade 6: Math 5AX, ETK · Reading G7K; Grade 7: Math Y8T, XDG · Reading XJK, BGN; Grade 8: Math CCR, 9T4 · Reading UJJ.

Ember video bridge: Take slow, deep breaths with the video. Breathe at a pace that feels comfortable. (Deep Breathing)

Reflection: Evaluate one group moment today. Did everyone have a real way to participate? What would you change? Everyone could participate when ___. Next time ___.

Look for: a concrete, respectful action. Reflections never ask about anyone’s disability or health; if a student has no real example, the fictional morning model is enough.

Tuesday October 27

Spark task: Analyze the group. What does each student use to communicate? Propose one group norm that makes sure every contribution counts.

Reveal / key: Speech, sign language, a picture board, and an AAC tablet. Strong norms: allow response time, do not finish someone’s sentence, ask before helping, and record ideas in more than one form.

Differentiate / facilitate: Push for a norm that changes what the group does, not just how members feel. AAC means augmentative and alternative communication.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What norm did you propose, and what would it change?” Turn-and-talk stem: “A norm we need is ___ because ___.”
