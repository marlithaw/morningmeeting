Why We Practice Expectations

Expectations aren’t taught by posting them—they’re taught by rehearsing them. This lesson turns Safe, Respectful, and Responsible into reps: students name the expectation, watch it modeled, practice the move, get feedback, and commit to one observable action in the setting where it is genuinely hard.

Under pressure, students fall to their level of practice. A calm, low-stakes rehearsal now is what holds up in the hallway, in group work, and during unstructured time later.

Grade-Band Practice Moves

Grades K–2

Keep it physical and warm. Children point, gesture, or echo. Model the right way, then have them show you; if it wobbles, rebuild clean. Pointing is a full answer.

Grades 3–5

Add a named setting and partner rehearsal. Students finish the sentence frame with a specific, observable action and rehearse it with a partner, adjusting on the second try.

Grades 6–8

Push for honesty and autonomy. Students privately choose the setting where they actually break the expectation and write one camera-confirmable rep: setting, action, and purpose.

Commitment Sentence Frames

Grades K–2
Today I will practice being ___.
Grades 3–5
Today, in ___, I will ___.
Grades 6–8
In ___, I will ___ so that ___.

Catch students being it during the day and name the specific action—recognition is evidence, not praise for a score.
