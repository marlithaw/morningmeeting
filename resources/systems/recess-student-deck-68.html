Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R3

“Anyone can follow rules when an adult is watching and there is a rule to follow. Free time is different — nobody is telling you what to do, so what you do is just who you are. That is not a threat. It is the most interesting thing about unstructured time.”

Look for

  • Students engage with the character framing without defensiveness
  • Students name what they actually do with free time

If it breaks

  • If it feels like a lecture: “What do you actually do out there? I am asking, not accusing.”

Support adult

  • Walk the perimeter. The students on the edges are the ones to know.

Language support

  • Newly arrived students are frequently on the perimeter, and it is often mistaken for preference.

Visual: Ball icon. Focus on the perimeter, not the games.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Four in, four hold, four out. The long exhale slows your heart rate through the vagus nerve. You do not have to believe in it. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine rather than performative participation
  • Resisters who join by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: “Sit quietly and let us work.” Refusal here is usually self-consciousness, not defiance.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry it.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' or say 'the nerve that controls heart rate.'

Visual: Lungs, clock, calm icons; optional heart-rate line dropping.

Slide 3 · Orient R3, R5

“You go where it is comfortable — everyone does, including me. The result is that the same people are on the edge every day. And this is one of the very few places where you have more power than we do. I cannot assign you friends. You can cross the gap.”

Look for

  • Students grasp their unique leverage here
  • Students identify the pattern without naming individuals

If it breaks

  • Redirect if it becomes about specific students.

Support adult

  • Never name an isolated student. It makes it permanently worse.

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'unstructured' and 'hierarchy.'

Visual: Three icons showing default, result, and intervention.

Slide 4 · Connect R3

“What keeps the gap open? Not who is in it — what holds it there. Skill gaps, language, being new, who picks, history between people. Name the mechanisms.”

Look for

  • Structural mechanisms identified
  • No individual students named

If it breaks

  • Redirect firmly if it becomes personal.

Support adult

  • Students will name real mechanisms you can actually change. Write them down.

Language support

  • Language is one of the mechanisms. Let multilingual students name it if they want to.

Visual: Two-people icon.

Slide 5 · Practice R3, R5

“'Do you want to play' makes them the person who had to be asked. 'We need one more' makes them useful. And do not make it a moment — no announcement, no look-what-I-did. That is how people humiliate someone while technically helping them.”

Look for

  • The reframed invitation being used
  • Invitations that stay casual

If it breaks

  • “That made it a moment. Keep it normal.”

Support adult

  • The casual delivery is the actual skill. Name it explicitly.

Language support

  • The exact script is provided.

Visual: Three icons with the phrasing distinction emphasized.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R3, R5

“One move. And keep it private — the moment you tell people you did it, it stops being for them and starts being for you.”

Look for

  • Private commitments
  • Perimeter thinning over a week

If it breaks

  • “Who is it actually for?”

Support adult

  • Observe the perimeter in a week

Language support

  • The script is provided.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we enter. A clean exit means the next class starts on time instead of burning ninety seconds. That is time you get back.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • Collective timing, no racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again, clean.”

Support adult

  • Position yourself at the destination

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the time.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock with the class's previous time.
