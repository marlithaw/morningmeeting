Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R5

“Everybody needs to use the bathroom. That is normal and nobody is ever in trouble for needing to go. Here is how we do it.” Keep your tone completely matter-of-fact.

Look for

  • Students relax
  • No giggling by the second reference — that means you normalized it

If it breaks

  • If there is silliness, stay flat and continue. Reacting makes it worse.

Support adult

  • Never make a child ask twice or explain why. Accidents at this age are frequently caused by adults delaying permission.

Language support

  • Establish a non-verbal signal so a child never has to ask in English.

Visual: Sink icon. Keep this matter-of-fact and warm — shame here does real damage.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Smell the flower. Blow out the candle. Long and slow. Three times.” Model it with them every single time — do not narrate from the front without doing it.

Look for

  • Exhale longer than inhale
  • Shoulders drop
  • Gesture copied

If it breaks

  • If silliness: model silently once, then invite them back. Do not lecture.

Support adult

  • Breathe audibly beside any student who cannot settle

Language support

  • No language demand — gesture carries it.
  • Same two gestures every day for ten days.

Visual: Flower and candle icons with matching hand gestures.

Slide 3 · Orient R1, R5

“Four things. Show me the signal. Go. Wash. Come back and sit down. Show me the signal right now so I know you know it.”

Look for

  • Every student can produce the signal
  • Students return without being retrieved

If it breaks

  • Re-teach the signal only.

Support adult

  • Approve the signal instantly, every time. Hesitation teaches children to stop asking.

Language support

  • The signal is non-verbal by design — that is the accessibility feature.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. Post inside the classroom near the door.

Slide 4 · Connect R2

“Show me the signal. Now wait for me to say yes. Then you go.” Practice it three times so it is automatic.

Look for

  • Signal produced without hesitation
  • Students wait for approval

If it breaks

  • Practice the signal again.

Support adult

  • Say yes quickly. Every time. This is the single most important adult behavior in this lesson.

Language support

  • Pointing and signaling require no English.

Visual: Three pictured steps of the signal sequence.

Slide 5 · Practice R1, R4

“Water. Soap — rub while we count to twenty together. Dry. Come back.” Count to twenty with them so the duration is concrete.

Look for

  • Full twenty counts
  • Students returning promptly

If it breaks

  • Re-teach the counting only.

Support adult

  • Post the icons above the sink at child height

Language support

  • Counting to twenty is accessible and gives a concrete duration.

Visual: Three handwashing icons. Post above the sink.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“Pick the one you forget most. Point to it.” Keep it light.

Look for

  • Every student points

If it breaks

  • Assign one.

Support adult

  • Never shame a child for any part of this routine, ever

Language support

  • Pointing is sufficient.

Visual: Three pictured promises.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we start our learning. Stand. Push in. Turn. Walk. Let's practice.” Do not time this at K–2.

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in unprompted
  • Calm bodies, not rushing

If it breaks

  • “Let's try that again. Back to your spot.” Rebuild clean, no commentary on individuals.

Support adult

  • Stand at the destination, not behind the group

Language support

  • Four icons, four words.
  • No timing pressure — it degrades the routine at this age.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. No stopwatch at this band.
