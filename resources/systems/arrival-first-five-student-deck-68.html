Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R5

“You have walked into a room that was already a mess and decided, before you sat down, that nothing was happening today. That is a real read and usually accurate. The entry is what caused it — and it is the cheapest thing in this building to fix.”

Look for

  • Students recognize the phenomenon from real experience
  • Students connect entry to their own disengagement

If it breaks

  • Ask which class has the worst entry and why. Take the answer seriously.

Support adult

  • Greet by name at the door. Adolescents notice its absence more than its presence.

Language support

  • Model before explaining.

Visual: Door icon. Teach at the actual threshold.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Four in, four hold, four out. The long exhale slows your heart rate through the vagus nerve. You do not have to believe in it. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine rather than performative participation
  • Resisters who join by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: “Sit quietly and let us work.” Refusal here is usually self-consciousness, not defiance.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry it.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' or say 'the nerve that controls heart rate.'

Visual: Lungs, clock, calm icons; optional heart-rate line dropping.

Slide 3 · Orient R1, R5

“Five moves and the fifth is the one everyone skips — arriving is not starting. And do the math: ninety seconds versus five minutes, times five days, is about fifteen minutes a week. That is yours either way. You just decide whether you spend it settling or learning.”

Look for

  • Students engage with the time math
  • Fifth step happening unprompted

If it breaks

  • “Back to the threshold. Clean run.” No commentary.

Support adult

  • Cue only the missed step

Language support

  • Five icons carry it. Numbers make the rationale accessible.

Visual: Five numbered step icons. Post at the door.

Slide 4 · Connect R2

“Which room has the best entry? And I want student behaviors, not 'that teacher is strict.' What do students actually DO in there?”

Look for

  • Student behaviors named rather than teacher traits
  • Students identifying transferable moves

If it breaks

  • If it becomes teacher评价: “Not the adult. What do the students do?”

Support adult

  • Take these observations to the named colleague — students are usually right about what works

Language support

  • Provide: ‘In ___ students ___ as soon as they walk in.’

Visual: Two-people icon.

Slide 5 · Practice R4, R5

“Everybody out. Yes, this feels ridiculous. Do it anyway — you cannot learn a physical routine from a chair. Three rounds, timed as a class.”

Look for

  • Time improves across rounds
  • Resistance dropping by round two

If it breaks

  • “Reset. Hallway.” Mechanical and neutral — never annoyed.

Support adult

  • Expect eye-rolling on round one. Ignore it. It disappears when the time drops.

Language support

  • Physical rehearsal is fully accessible.

Visual: Three rehearsal icons plus a clock.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“Name the step, the class where it breaks worst, and the actual fix. If the fix is 'pack my bag the night before,' say that — it is a real answer.”

Look for

  • Specific class named
  • Fix matches the named weak step

If it breaks

  • “Which class is worst? Start there.”

Support adult

  • Reference the commitment at the door next class

Language support

  • Offer a verb bank: pause, stow, prep, start.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we enter. A clean exit means the next class starts on time instead of burning ninety seconds. That is time you get back.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • Collective timing, no racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again, clean.”

Support adult

  • Position yourself at the destination

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the time.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock with the class's previous time.
