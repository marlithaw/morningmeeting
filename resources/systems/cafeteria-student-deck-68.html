Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R3

“Nobody assigns lunch seats. Which means the map of who sits where is the truest picture of this school that exists — truer than anything on our walls. And you drew that map. Which means you can redraw it.”

Look for

  • Students engage with the map framing
  • Students name the actual social geography honestly

If it breaks

  • Have them literally draw the cafeteria map. It is more honest than discussion.

Support adult

  • The map will show you cliques, isolation, and often racial or linguistic sorting. Do not look away from it.

Language support

  • Newly arrived students are frequently isolated at lunch. The map makes it undeniable.

Visual: Tray icon. Consider having students actually map the cafeteria.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Four in, four hold, four out. The long exhale slows your heart rate through the vagus nerve. You do not have to believe in it. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine rather than performative participation
  • Resisters who join by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: “Sit quietly and let us work.” Refusal here is usually self-consciousness, not defiance.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry it.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' or say 'the nerve that controls heart rate.'

Visual: Lungs, clock, calm icons; optional heart-rate line dropping.

Slide 3 · Orient R3, R5

“Sitting with who is comfortable is human. But the cost lands on the same person every single day. And here is the part worth hearing — isolation at school predicts dropping out and depression. Adults cannot fix that. We cannot assign friends. You are the only people who can do this.”

Look for

  • Students grasp that this is uniquely theirs to solve
  • Students name a specific isolated peer — privately

If it breaks

  • If it becomes performative pity: “This is not charity. It is a seat.”

Support adult

  • Never name an isolated student publicly. That makes it worse, permanently.

Language support

  • Pre-teach 'isolation' and 'predictor.'

Visual: Three icons tracking comfort, cost, and the intervention.

Slide 4 · Connect R3

“Draw it. Who sits where and what decides it. Then the real question — who did you not draw? Who is not on your map at all? That is the person this whole lesson is about.”

Look for

  • Honest mapping including sorting by race, language, or grade
  • Students realizing they omitted someone

If it breaks

  • “Look at your map. Now count the people in this grade. Where is everyone else?”

Support adult

  • Collect the maps. They are the best social data you will get all year. Keep them confidential.

Language support

  • Drawing removes the language demand entirely.

Visual: Two-people icon. Provide paper for an actual map.

Slide 5 · Practice R3, R5

“Look up in the first two minutes. Then say the actual words out loud — 'there's a seat here.' A vague head-nod is not an invitation and everyone knows it. And do it without making it a whole thing. Low key is what makes it land.”

Look for

  • The exact phrase used
  • Invitations that are casual rather than performative

If it breaks

  • “That was a gesture. Say the words.”

Support adult

  • Warn them: making it a big deal humiliates the person. Casual is the whole skill.

Language support

  • The four-word script is fully accessible and is often the first English a new student hears from a peer.

Visual: Three icons with the exact invitation script emphasized.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R3, R5

“One thing this week. And keep it to yourself — the second it becomes a project you are announcing, it stops being an invitation and starts being charity. Nobody wants to be someone's good deed.”

Look for

  • Private, low-key commitments
  • Actual seat changes visible within a week

If it breaks

  • “Would you want to be someone's project? Then do not make anyone yours.”

Support adult

  • Observe the cafeteria in a week. The map will have moved.

Language support

  • The script is provided.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we enter. A clean exit means the next class starts on time instead of burning ninety seconds. That is time you get back.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • Collective timing, no racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again, clean.”

Support adult

  • Position yourself at the destination

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the time.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock with the class's previous time.
