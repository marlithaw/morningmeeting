Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R3

“Lunch has almost no structure, which is exactly why it is where you find out whether you belong here. Everyone in this room knows what it feels like to look for a seat.”

Look for

  • Recognition — most students have experienced this
  • Students naming the seat-finding moment

If it breaks

  • Name your own experience of looking for a seat.

Support adult

  • Watch the first two minutes of lunch. That is when exclusion happens.

Language support

  • Newly arrived students experience this most acutely. Pair them deliberately.

Visual: Tray icon. This lesson is about belonging first and procedure second.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“In for four, out for six. The breath OUT is the one that calms you — that is the whole trick. Three rounds.” Count aloud with them.

Look for

  • Exhale visibly longer
  • Shoulders drop by round two

If it breaks

  • “This one is for you, not for me. Eyes down, one more round.”

Support adult

  • Co-regulate beside students who struggle

Language support

  • Counting is the only demand.
  • Model rather than describe.

Visual: Lungs and calm-face icons; optional 4/6 count bar.

Slide 3 · Orient R3, R5

“The procedure is easy. The real job is noticing who has nowhere to sit and making room before they have to ask. Asking is the hard part — if someone has to ask, you already missed it.”

Look for

  • Students grasp that the invitation must come before the ask
  • Students name who tends to be alone

If it breaks

  • “Have you ever had to ask? How did that feel?”

Support adult

  • Track who eats alone across a week. The pattern is stable and fixable.

Language support

  • Provide: ‘There is a seat here.’ Four words that change a student's week.

Visual: Two icons with the second visually emphasized.

Slide 4 · Connect R3

“What is actually hardest about lunch? For a lot of people it is the first two minutes — walking in and scanning for a seat.”

Look for

  • Students name the seat-scan
  • Students recognize it is hardest for new students

If it breaks

  • Name the first-two-minutes phenomenon directly.

Support adult

  • This tells you exactly where to position adults

Language support

  • Word bank covers both halves.

Visual: Two-people icon with word bank.

Slide 5 · Practice R3, R5

“Look up in the first two minutes — that is when it happens. Then say the actual words: 'There's a seat here.' Do not wave vaguely. Say it. Four words.”

Look for

  • Students looking up rather than straight to their group
  • The exact phrase being used

If it breaks

  • Practice the phrase aloud as a class. It feels awkward once, then it is automatic.

Support adult

  • Watch the first two minutes tomorrow and note who looks up

Language support

  • The exact four-word script is the scaffold and is fully accessible.

Visual: Three icons. The invitation phrase must be taught as an exact script.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R3, R5

“One thing, today. Looking up counts — that is not a small commitment, it is the whole thing.”

Look for

  • Inclusion-oriented commitments
  • Follow-through visible at lunch

If it breaks

  • “Who is going to benefit?”

Support adult

  • Observe at lunch and name it privately afterward

Language support

  • Word bank covers the first blank.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave the meeting is how we enter the learning. We time it as one class, against our own record — not against each other.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • No pushing or racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again until it is clean.”

Support adult

  • Redirect to the step, not the behavior

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the class time — numbers are accessible.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock showing the class's previous time.
