Your teacher · inside
Front and center. Says the hallway script, checks every computer, and releases the line only when everyone is ready.
Restorative team · in the hall
Outside the doors with radios. Says the same words, watches voice 0, the right side, and hands, and checks computers and dress code.

Slide 1 · Title & Promise

“Starting now, every hallway transition in grades 5 through 8 runs the same way. Six expectations, numbered one through six. I say them in here before the door opens. The restorative team says the same words in the hall. Same words, every adult, every time.”

Look for

  • Students hear that this is the same in every room, not a rule from one teacher
  • Students notice the six expectations are numbered

If it breaks

  • If students push back (“this is extra”): “It's the same for everybody. That's the point.” Then move on.

Support adult

  • Grade 5 rooms (205, 207, 209) follow this same procedure. This deck works for 5th grade too.
  • Have Middle School Hallway Transitions open for your grade's room moves.

Language support

  • Name the three adults with gestures: “me, in here,” “the team, out there,” “your next teacher, in there.”

Slide 2 · Regulate

“Four in, four hold, four out. The long exhale slows your heart rate through the vagus nerve. You do not have to believe in it. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine rather than performative participation
  • Resisters who join by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: “Sit quietly and let us work.” Refusal here is usually self-consciousness, not defiance.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry it.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' or say 'the nerve that controls heart rate.'

Slide 3 · Orient · The Six

“Toes pointed in the direction you're going. Hands to yourself and your space. Voice level 0, silent in the hall. Computer closed, held safely with two hands. Walk on the right and stay on the line. Walk in and start the First Five on the screen. Say the numbers back with me.” Call out a number, have students call back the expectation.

Look for

  • Students can name the expectation from the number alone
  • Computer closed and two hands, not under an arm or open

If it breaks

  • “Stop. Number 4. Show me two hands.” Then call the next number.

Support adult

  • Use these exact words. The restorative team uses them in the hall. If adults say it differently, students hear two rules.

Language support

  • Pair each number with a gesture: point toes, hands in, finger to lips, two hands up, point right, point to the screen.
  • Pre-teach “direction” and “stay on the line.”

Slide 4 · Orient · Who Does What

“I stay right here, front and center. I do not walk into the hall. The restorative team is outside the doors with radios, saying the same words I just said. Your next teacher is inside their room, front and center, with the First Five already on the screen. Your job is the space in between.”

Look for

  • Students understand the sending teacher does not walk the line
  • Students know the First Five is waiting on the screen every time

If it breaks

  • If students ask who is “in charge” in the hall: “The restorative team. Same words, same expectations.”

Support adult

  • Receiving teachers: project the First Five 100% of the time (materials, voice level, exactly what to do, timer).
  • 5th grade: there is no restorative team. Teachers run their own transitions, one room at a time.

Language support

  • Point to the door, the hallway, and the next room as you name each adult.

Slide 5 · Orient · The Redirection Ladder

“Here's what happens in the hall. First, the adult names who's doing it right. If you need a reminder, you'll get a silent cue, like a finger to the lips, a point to the right side, or a number held up. Then maybe just a number: ‘Number 3.’ Fix it and keep walking. If you step out, you're not in trouble. We practice it and you go to class. A reset in the Restorative Room means you fill out the form and get right back to learning.”

Look for

  • Students hear stepping out as practice, not punishment
  • Students know the line keeps moving

If it breaks

  • If a student argues in practice: “I hear you. Right now, I need number 3. We can talk after.”

Support adult

  • Restorative team: praise more than you correct (3 to 1). Same words, every adult. Small and private. Keep the line moving. Stay calm.
  • Rehearse up to 10 times, then “That's exactly it. Let's go to class.” A reset is calm, never a threat.

Language support

  • Act out a silent cue and a number cue so students see the difference.

Slide 6 · Connect

“Turn to your partner. What changes when every adult says the same words? And which number is going to be the hardest for this class? Be honest. Restate your partner's answer before you respond.”

Look for

  • Students connect the same words to fairness (no one gets singled out)
  • A specific number named as hardest, with a reason

If it breaks

  • Offer the stem: “The hardest one for us is number ___ because ___.”

Support adult

  • Note the number students name. That's the one to narrate first in practice.

Language support

  • Sentence stem posted.
  • Students can answer with a number and a gesture.

Slide 7 · Practice

Say the full hallway script exactly: “Stand up and push in your chair. Computer closed, held with two hands. Hold it up so I can see it. Hallway expectations: toes pointed where we're going, hands to yourself, voice level zero. We walk on the right, straight into Room ___, and start the First Five on the screen. Eyes on me, voices off. Let's go.”

Look for

  • A full line of computers in hand. Look at every student before you release.
  • Students walk straight in and start the First Five

If it breaks

  • Do not release the line until everyone is ready.
  • At the receiving door: students who don't enter well wait by the door and practice again after everyone is started.

Support adult

  • 6th: Room 238 comes out and waits along the right side. 237 walks into 238. 235 comes around into 237. The waiting 238 students walk into 235.
  • 7th: Room 232 waits at the stairway behind the blue line, against the lockers and wall. 236 walks into 232. The team member from 236 escorts 222 into 236. The team member at 222 sends the waiting 232 class into 222.
  • 8th: Room 234 lines up against the lockers at the stairway. 233 walks into 234. The team member from 233 escorts 231 into 233. The team member at 231 sends the waiting 234 class into 231.

Language support

  • The ready check is physical: two hands up shows understanding without words.

Rehearse in the real hallway with the restorative team in position. 5th grade: one room moves at a time; room moves are still to be confirmed.

Slide 8 · Commit

“Pick the number you're most likely to hear in the hall. Not the easy one. Then tell me what I'll actually see when you're holding it.”

Look for

  • A specific number
  • An observable action (“two hands on my computer,” “I'm on the right side”)

If it breaks

  • If the answer is “I won't get in trouble”: “What will I see you doing? Say the action.”

Support adult

  • Narrate commitments you see at the next transition: “I see number 4, two hands. Thank you.”

Language support

  • Students may answer with the number plus a gesture.

Slide 9 · Transition

Run the real transition with the full hallway script. Release only when every computer is closed and up in two hands. Then: “Eyes on me, voices off. Let's go.”

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in unprompted
  • Every computer closed and held with two hands

If it breaks

  • Hold the release until the line is ready. Do not walk into the hall.

Support adult

  • Receiving teacher: point to the screen and narrate the positive: “I see Room ___ walking in silently and getting started. Thank you.” Check the seating chart during the First Five.

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • The First Five on the screen shows exactly what to do.
