Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R3

“Lunch is for eating and for being together. Everybody has a seat. Nobody eats alone unless they want to.” Say the seat line clearly — it matters more than the procedure.

Look for

  • Students hear the belonging message
  • Students notice who sits alone

If it breaks

  • Repeat the seat line.

Support adult

  • Watch for children who sit alone. That is the real data from this lesson.

Language support

  • The belonging message matters most for newly arrived students.

Visual: Tray icon. 'Everybody has a seat' is the most important line here.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Smell the flower. Blow out the candle. Long and slow. Three times.” Model it with them every single time — do not narrate from the front without doing it.

Look for

  • Exhale longer than inhale
  • Shoulders drop
  • Gesture copied

If it breaks

  • If silliness: model silently once, then invite them back. Do not lecture.

Support adult

  • Breathe audibly beside any student who cannot settle

Language support

  • No language demand — gesture carries it.
  • Same two gestures every day for ten days.

Visual: Flower and candle icons with matching hand gestures.

Slide 3 · Orient R1, R5

“Line up. Get food. Sit down. Small voice. Show me small voice right now.” Practice the volume before you get there.

Look for

  • Students can produce the small voice on cue
  • Order held in the actual cafeteria

If it breaks

  • Re-teach the voice level only.

Support adult

  • Practice the volume in the classroom first — the cafeteria is too loud to teach in

Language support

  • Four icons carry the sequence.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. Post at the cafeteria entrance.

Slide 4 · Connect R3

“You can sit with a friend. You can sit with someone new. Point to who you will sit with today.” Notice who picks 'someone new.'

Look for

  • Some students choose someone new
  • Students notice peers sitting alone

If it breaks

  • Model it: “Today I am going to sit with someone I do not usually sit with.”

Support adult

  • Track who sits alone repeatedly and intervene warmly

Language support

  • Pointing is a complete answer. This slide matters most for new arrivals.

Visual: Three pictured seating options. 'Someone new' is the one to emphasize.

Slide 5 · Practice R4, R5

“Tray stacked. Wipe your spot. Line up. The class after us should not be able to tell we were here.”

Look for

  • Tables actually wiped
  • Trays stacked rather than dropped

If it breaks

  • “Back to your seats. We are cleaning it again.”

Support adult

  • Check the tables before dismissing

Language support

  • Three icons, three actions.

Visual: Three cleanup icons. Post above the tray return.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R3, R5

“Pick one. Point to it.” Notice out loud when you see the 'someone new' one happen.

Look for

  • Every student points
  • Someone actually sits with a new person

If it breaks

  • Assign one.

Support adult

  • Name it when you see the inclusion happen

Language support

  • Pointing is sufficient.

Visual: Three pictured promises.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we start our learning. Stand. Push in. Turn. Walk. Let's practice.” Do not time this at K–2.

Look for

  • Chairs pushed in unprompted
  • Calm bodies, not rushing

If it breaks

  • “Let's try that again. Back to your spot.” Rebuild clean, no commentary on individuals.

Support adult

  • Stand at the destination, not behind the group

Language support

  • Four icons, four words.
  • No timing pressure — it degrades the routine at this age.

Visual: Four numbered step icons. No stopwatch at this band.
