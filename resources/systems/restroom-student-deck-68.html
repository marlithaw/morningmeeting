Slide 1 · Title & Promise R1, R5

“You should never have to explain to an adult why you need a bathroom. That is not a thing. The routine exists for two reasons — your privacy, and keeping the restroom itself safe. Not to control you.”

Look for

  • Students take the framing seriously
  • Reduced testing of the routine once the rationale is honest

If it breaks

  • If students are cynical: “Has an adult ever made you explain? Then you know why I am saying this.”

Support adult

  • If your building has restroom restrictions, be honest about them and about why. Pretending otherwise costs you everything.

Language support

  • Non-verbal signal removes the language barrier at an urgent moment.

Visual: Sink icon. Be direct and unembarrassed. Adolescents detect adult discomfort immediately.

Slide 2 · Regulate R1

“Four in, four hold, four out. The long exhale slows your heart rate through the vagus nerve. You do not have to believe in it. Three rounds.”

Look for

  • Genuine rather than performative participation
  • Resisters who join by day three — that is the win

If it breaks

  • If a student refuses: “Sit quietly and let us work.” Refusal here is usually self-consciousness, not defiance.

Support adult

  • Participate visibly. Adolescents read adult non-participation instantly.

Language support

  • Numbers carry it.
  • Pre-teach 'vagus nerve' or say 'the nerve that controls heart rate.'

Visual: Lungs, clock, calm icons; optional heart-rate line dropping.

Slide 3 · Orient R1, R5

“Signal, go, wash, return. And here is the honest reason it is short: the restroom is the least supervised place in this building. Short trips are what keep it safe. That is not about trusting you — it is about density and time.”

Look for

  • Students engage with the safety rationale rather than reading it as suspicion
  • Prompt returns

If it breaks

  • Re-teach the return expectation only.

Support adult

  • The honest rationale works far better than a rule stated without one at this age

Language support

  • Signal is non-verbal by design.

Visual: Four numbered step icons.

Slide 4 · Connect R2

“What makes a restroom feel unsafe here? And what would actually fix it? I want real answers — I will take them to the people who can change things.”

Look for

  • Honest answers about crowding, vaping, conflict, or avoidance
  • Students who avoid restrooms entirely naming that

If it breaks

  • If students are guarded, offer a written option instead.

Support adult

  • Some students avoid school restrooms all day. That is a health issue. Take what they say seriously and act on it.

Language support

  • Offer a written or drawn response option.

Visual: Two-people icon.

Slide 5 · Practice R4, R5

“Silent signal. In and out. And leave it the way you would want to walk into it. Be clear about the mechanism here — restroom restrictions get added when trips get long and things get destroyed. That is how it actually happens, everywhere.”

Look for

  • Silent signals
  • Short trips
  • Restroom condition improving

If it breaks

  • Address duration privately, never publicly.

Support adult

  • Never discuss an individual's restroom use in front of peers. Ever.

Language support

  • Signal is non-verbal.

Visual: Three icons covering signal, duration, condition.

Slide 6 · Commit R2, R5

“One thing that keeps this private and safe for everybody — including the people who currently avoid going all day.”

Look for

  • Commitments oriented to the group
  • Recognition that some peers avoid restrooms entirely

If it breaks

  • “Who does this affect besides you?”

Support adult

  • Reinforce quietly. Never publicly.

Language support

  • Offer a verb bank: signal, return, leave, report.

Visual: Check icon.

Slide 7 · Transition R1, R4, R5

“How we leave is how we enter. A clean exit means the next class starts on time instead of burning ninety seconds. That is time you get back.”

Look for

  • Chairs in unprompted
  • Collective timing, no racing

If it breaks

  • “Reset. First thirty seconds again, clean.”

Support adult

  • Position yourself at the destination

Language support

  • Icons carry the sequence.
  • Post the time.

Visual: Four step icons plus a clock with the class's previous time.
