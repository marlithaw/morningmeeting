Screen 1 · Welcome · 0:00

Target: Write a plan with when and where for each action, and one backup if the plan is interrupted.

Script: "Yesterday we looked back at how far we have come. Today we look ahead. Fall break is almost here. A break is a pause, not a stop. The people who come back strongest are the ones who had a plan for the pause." Look for: Students connect today to yesterday. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 2 · Regulate · 2:00

Prepare: One My Break Plan card and one Calm Tools to Take Home card per student.

Script: "Early-warning check. Shoulders. Jaw. Hands. Breath. Notice which one is holding tension. Release it. Your body gives you warnings before you lose your cool. Over break, nobody will remind you to check. That is why you practice it here."

Facilitation: These are the Morning Circle check-in tools from the first week of September. Name them by name. Look for: Students settle and notice their body without being asked to report out. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 3 · Orient · 2:00

Model "Rest: phone off at 10. Safe: tell an adult where I'm going before I leave. Respectful: when my little cousin bugs me, I use the early-warning check before I snap. Responsible: 15 minutes of IXL or reading a day, right after lunch."

Script: "Here is the truth about breaks: the values don't take a vacation. Safe, Respectful, and Responsible still matter when nobody is checking. Add Rest, because recharging is a skill too. A plan is not a wish. A plan is what plus when."

Check: "Is 'Be good over break' a plan?" Expected: No. It is a wish. A plan names an action. Look for: Students can name the four parts. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 4 · Connect · Look · 2:00

Worked response "Eli's lines name what, when, and the situation. Nia's lines name a feeling or a hope. You can't check whether 'be nice' happened. You can check whether you asked for the charger instead of yelling."

If a student is unsure: "A wish says _. A plan says _ and when."

Script: "Don't decide who is a good kid. Look only at the plans. Which plan has actions you could actually see happen?" Give twenty seconds of silent looking, then take two or three answers. Facilitation: Breaks look different for every family. Some families travel. Some work every day. Some stay home. Every plan counts. Name that out loud if any student compares trips or gifts. Look for: Students point to time and action words as the difference. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 5 · Connect · Share · 1:00

Response options Either frame is fine. Students may name something small: sleeping in, a game, cooking, a show, time outside.

Script: "Breaks can feel exciting. For some people, breaks feel long. Both are normal. Share one thing you are looking forward to, or one thing that will help you. Partner A, then Partner B. A pass is okay." Facilitation: Do not ask where students are going or what they are getting. Look for: Students share without comparing. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 6 · Practice · 3:00

Three-minute facilitation 0:00 to 0:30: point to the when and where words. 0:30 to 2:30: students complete the plan and the backup line. 2:30 to 3:00: partners trade one line and ask, "When exactly?"

Look for: Actions are concrete. 6–8 lines have a time or place. If the response misses the skill: If a line has no time, ask, "When? After what? Before what?"

Script: "Now build your plan. One action in each box. This card goes home with you today. Put it somewhere you will see it: the fridge, your mirror, your backpack." Facilitation: Send My Break Plan cards and Calm Tools to Take Home cards home today in backpacks. Look for: Every box has an action. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 7 · Commit · 3:00

If support is needed: See the response support on the Practice screen. Commitment support: Offer one action from the student's own card; ask them to say, draw, dictate, or write when they will practice it today. Extend when ready: "Which part of your plan will be hardest to keep? What will help?"

Script: "A plan works better when you practice it before you need it. Pick one part of your plan you can practice today, right here at school. Being Respectful at lunch. Being Responsible with your materials. Whisper it to your partner." Look for: Commitments connect to something that happens today at school. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Screen 8 · Transition · 2:00

Expected exit response "A plan says what and when. I know which part I care about most."

Mastery evidence: Students can name the four parts and give one specific action.

Script: "Tomorrow we get ready to come back. Your plan goes home today. Stand on the one." Look for: A calm transition into the first task. Access: Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Practice boundary: Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.
