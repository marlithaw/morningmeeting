Monday October 5 · Grades 6–8 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

The Year So Far timeline card (toolkit) and one Look Back Card per student.

Say and model

"Proud: our class transitions in under a minute now; in August it took three. That is evidence. Learned: the 4 Ds. Next: our growth edge is sustaining focus for the full Do Now."

Three-minute practice

0:00 to 0:30: point to the evidence line. 0:30 to 2:30: students complete the card. 2:30 to 3:00: volunteers read a Proud line with its evidence.

Worked answer

"The First Five shows up most. Evidence: most of us start the Do Now in under a minute now." "Ask → Wait → Respect. Evidence: people ask before grabbing someone's stuff more than they did in August."

Response scaffold

"The skill I see most is _. One moment I saw it was _."

Look for

Every box has something. Proud lines are specific. Next lines are honest.

If the response misses the skill

If evidence is missing, ask, "What would someone have seen? When and where?"

Extend when ready

"What is one tool from this year you could teach to someone at home?"

Expected exit response

"I can prove one way I have grown and I know my next step."

Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Tuesday October 6 · Grades 6–8 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

One My Break Plan card and one Calm Tools to Take Home card per student.

Say and model

"Rest: phone off at 10. Safe: tell an adult where I'm going before I leave. Respectful: when my little cousin bugs me, I use the early-warning check before I snap. Responsible: 15 minutes of IXL or reading a day, right after lunch."

Three-minute practice

0:00 to 0:30: point to the when and where words. 0:30 to 2:30: students complete the plan and the backup line. 2:30 to 3:00: partners trade one line and ask, "When exactly?"

Worked answer

"Eli's lines name what, when, and the situation. Nia's lines name a feeling or a hope. You can't check whether 'be nice' happened. You can check whether you asked for the charger instead of yelling."

Response scaffold

"A wish says _. A plan says _ and when."

Look for

Actions are concrete. 6–8 lines have a time or place.

If the response misses the skill

If a line has no time, ask, "When? After what? Before what?"

Extend when ready

"Which part of your plan will be hardest to keep? What will help?"

Expected exit response

"A plan says what and when. I know which part I care about most."

Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.

Wednesday October 7 · Grades 6–8 · Teacher companion

Model · Prompt · Check

Have ready

One Comeback Card per student and the Comeback Folder label.

Say and model

"Ready body: two nights before, phone off at 10. Ready routine: Do Now in 60 every class the first day. Ready goal: raise my reading score. Measure: finish 3 IXL skills a week."

Three-minute practice

0:00 to 0:30: point to the "I'll know because" line. 0:30 to 2:30: students complete the card. 2:30 to 3:00: partners check each other's measure: "Could you count that?"

Worked answer

"Jada came back ready. Evidence: seated, started in 40 seconds, has a goal. Leo showed up. He was on time, but his routine and goal were not ready. Talking to friends is not wrong; the timing is. Break stories belong at Morning Meeting or lunch."

Response scaffold

"_ came back ready because _."

Look for

Goals are specific. First steps happen in the first week back.

If the response misses the skill

If there is no measure, ask, "How will you know you got there? What would you count?"

Extend when ready

"What could get in the way of your goal? What will you do then?"

Expected exit response

"Coming back ready means my sleep, my routine, and my goal are set before the first day."

Offer written frames and quiet thinking time. Students may pass. Ask for evidence: when, where, what it looked like.

Breaks look different for every family. Plans and reflections stay private unless the student chooses to share.
