Teach Back and Ready

Monday through Thursday, every band practices instead of listens: Monday rehearses Safe, Respectful, and Responsible in arrival, the hallway, and the cafeteria; Tuesday practices the testing routine right before Checkpoint 2; Wednesday carries the same expectations into new rooms, new teachers, and transitions; Thursday builds the effort to finish strong. Every lesson uses the same move: model it, rehearse it, fix it, own it. K–2 rooms teach the day’s Lee &amp; Tee lesson this week; the K–2 deck is ready if a room prefers it. Friday’s Morning Spark (Friday Wonder) carries the same theme, followed by Friday Cypher.

Timing: Welcome ready screen; Regulate 2, Orient 2, Connect 3 (Look 2 and Share 1), Practice 3, Commit 3, Transition 2 minutes.

Respect and privacy, every day: practice is about the routine, never about a student who struggled with it before the break. No one is named as the example of what not to do. Test nerves are normal and are never shamed; students share only what they choose, and no one shares a score, a goal number, or a result. If a student shares worry about testing or about the changes, say: “Thank you for telling me. Let’s talk after,” and follow up privately. Welcome responses by writing, drawing, speaking, pointing, signing, or a student’s usual communication tool, and in any language.

Video clips: each “Watch 60 seconds” button opens the YouTube video with captions on and stops at one minute. If a video will not load, read the key message aloud a second time and continue.

Participation moves for every lesson

Equity sticks (cold call): keep one stick per student in a “not yet called” cup. Always give think time first, then say, “Everyone has an answer. I will pull a stick.” Pull, read the name, ask the question, and move the stick to the “called” cup. If a student needs a moment, say “I will come back to you,” call one more student, and return. Accept pointing, signing, or a device response. The scripts name exactly when to pull a stick.

Turn and talk: partners A and B. “Partner A answers first. Partner B, listen so you can repeat it.” After 30 seconds: “Switch.” Then cold call: “Tell us what your partner said.” K–2 partners sit knee to knee for 10 to 15 seconds.

Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “Do you agree or disagree? Why?” “Say it in your own words.” “What in the video or slide makes you think that?”

LiveSchool points: award during the work, name the Safe, Respectful, or Responsible behavior out loud, then tap. Each lesson script includes the exact words. This week, connect points to routines students can see: a clean, quiet line, walking on the right, a calm cafeteria voice, materials ready, eyes on the work, and a strong try on a hard problem. About four positives for every correction. Never award points for being quiet or for finishing first.

Behavior supports: keep the lesson moving. Give one short reminder that names the expectation, then tally it and keep teaching. Each day’s Transition note has the exact words for the most likely disruption. Rehearsals can get silly; when one does, stop, model the routine once the right way, and run it again (“Let’s do it again, the right way”). A redo is practice, not a punishment. Safety comes first: any unsafe behavior gets immediate adult support.

Monday November 30 · Grades K–2 · Indiana SEL 2A Self-Management · 6A Responsible Decision-Making

Reset Our Expectations

Target: I can show safe, respectful, and responsible at school.

Key message: Welcome back! We remember our school rules by doing them. Walking feet in the hall. A quiet voice in line. Hands to ourselves. Our things in our cubby. Let’s practice.

Prepare: Safe, Respectful, Responsible page from the K–2 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Sesame Street: Raise Your Hand Song (Sesame Street).

Evidence: The child colors a picture for each rule and shows one rule with their body.

Differentiate: Kindergarten: point to each picture while an adult names the rule. Grade 1: say “I am safe when I ___.” Grade 2: write one rule under its picture.

Answer notes: Safe: walking feet. Respectful: quiet voice, raising a hand. Responsible: putting things away. Any safe, respectful, or responsible action the child shows is correct.

Take home: School Rules. Family chat: Show your family how we walk in line at school.

Answer notes: Responsible: the student who sat right away. A redo: sit down, calm voice, eyes on the adult, no arguing. Any specific, observable action in the chosen setting is correct (for example, Hallway · Safe: walk on the right).

Reset Our Expectations

Target: I can rehearse the expectation where I actually slip and commit to one action a camera could see.

Key message: Under pressure, people fall to their level of practice. After a break, everyone’s routines slip a little. The cafeteria and the hallway are where it shows: standing up, getting loud, not following a direction the first time. Strong students do not wait to be corrected. They reset on their own.

Prepare: My Reset Rep page from the 6–8 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: SOAR Focus: Hallway Transitions (Alice Drive Middle School).

Evidence: The student privately names a real setting and writes one observable rep with setting, action, and purpose.

Differentiate: Grade 6: use the chips to build the rep. Grades 7–8: explain what an audience does to a cafeteria or hallway moment and how a reset protects you. Accept oral responses.

Answer notes: The student who sat and said “Okay” is back with the class; following first kept the reset short. Arguing or walking off escalates. A strong rep: “In the hallway, I will walk on the right with my voice low so that we get to class on time and nobody gets stopped.”

Take home: Reset Rep. Family chat: What helps you get back into a routine after time off?

Tuesday December 1 · Grades 3–5 · Indiana SEL 2A Self-Management · 2B Self-Management

Ready to Focus

Target: I can use a testing routine: calm start, a plan for hard questions, and quiet focus until everyone is done.

Key message: Feeling nervous before a test is normal. It means you care. A testing routine helps: take three slow breaths, read each question carefully, and when one is hard, use your plan instead of giving up. When you finish, check your work, then stay quiet so everyone can finish too.

Prepare: My Testing Routine page from the 3–5 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Test Anxiety Strategies For Kids (Mental Health Center Kids).

Evidence: The student practices three slow breaths and writes a hard-question plan with at least two steps plus a helpful self-talk sentence.

Differentiate: Grade 3: build the plan with the chips. Grades 4–5: explain why rushing to finish hurts your score more than a hard question does. Students may draw and dictate.

Answer notes: Helpful self-talk: “This is hard, and I can try,” or “One question at a time.” A strong plan: breathe, reread, cross out wrong answers, choose the best answer, move on, and check at the end.

Take home: Testing Routine. Family chat: What helps you stay calm when something is hard?

Finish Strong

Target: I can explain how effort changes my brain and choose to finish strong when I am tired.

Key message: When you push through something hard, your brain builds stronger connections. That is why the end of a long task matters: it is where the growth happens. Finishing strong is not about being the smartest. It is about not quitting on yourself when you are tired, bored, or stuck.

Prepare: The Last Mile page from the 6–8 toolkit (one per student); pencils and crayons; equity sticks; LiveSchool open; Show-Me cards from the toolkit. Video: Growth Mindset (4 of 5): The Mysterious World of Neurons (ClassDojo).

Evidence: The student explains that effort strengthens brain connections and writes a specific, observable finish-strong plan.

Differentiate: Grade 6: use the chips for the plan. Grades 7–8: connect the neuron idea to a skill you have improved through practice (a sport, music, a game). Accept oral responses.

Answer notes: Effort on hard tasks strengthens connections between neurons. Isaiah is missing practice and class time. A strong plan: start the Do Now, one real effort per class, reset instead of shutting down, ask for help when stuck.

Take home: The Last Mile. Family chat: Who do you know that finishes what they start? How do they do it?
