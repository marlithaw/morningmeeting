Every Do Now: Laps and Marks

Tomorrow is Friday Cypher.

Matchbook Morning Meeting · The First FiveGrades 1–2 · Thursday

Thursday · Welcome · Ready screen + video

Run the room’s usual greeting.

Select Watch the First 5 on this slide to play the Relax · First 5 video. Before it starts: “This is what the First Five looks like when it is working. Watch what the teacher does while they work.” After it ends, take one answer.

“While you keep going, I walk a path around the room. I do not talk. I leave a mark on your paper. Each mark is a message. Let us learn all four.”

Look for

  • Students are curious about the marks
  • Students name one thing they saw in the video

If it breaks

  • Keep the hook to one minute.

Support adult

  • Name yesterday’s live rep: who kept going.

Language support

  • The picture carries the idea.

Visual: Teacher icon on red.

Thursday · Regulate · 1:00

“Smell the flower. Blow the candle. Long and slow. Three times.” Breathe with students every time.

Look for

  • Breath out is longer than breath in
  • Shoulders drop

If it breaks

  • If it gets silly, model it once in silence and start again.

Support adult

  • Breathe beside any student who needs help settling.

Language support

  • The gesture carries the meaning.

Visual: Flower and candle icons.

Thursday · Orient · 2:00 · Option S: Structured Marks

Teach each mark. Students draw it in the air.

“Where the mark is tells you something too. A check on the side is about you. A star or a circle on your answer is about your work.”

Look for

  • Students draw each mark in the air
  • Students can say what each mark means

If it breaks

  • Go back to one mark and teach it again.

Support adult

  • Point to the anchor chart on the wall.

Language support

  • The marks carry meaning without words.

Visual: The marks, large, with where each goes.

Thursday · Orient · 2:00 · Option R: Our Room’s Own Marks

Point to the room’s posted chart. Teach each mark in the order it appears: what it looks like, where it goes on the paper, and what students do when they see it. Students draw each one in the air.

Look for

  • Students can say what each room mark means

If it breaks

  • Go back to one mark and teach it again.

Support adult

  • Stand beside the chart while teaching.

Language support

  • The room’s chart carries the meaning.

Visual: Arrow pointing toward the room’s chart.

Thursday · Connect · 2:00 · Option S: Structured Marks

Point to one mark at a time, in random order. “Hold up 1 for check, 2 for star, 3 for circle, 4 for dot. Then tell your partner what you would do.” Eight quick rounds.

Look for

  • Fast, correct responses
  • Every student responds each round

If it breaks

  • Slow down and do two rounds together.

Support adult

  • Stand beside students still learning the marks.

Language support

  • Fingers and motions are full answers.

Visual: The marks with a response for each.

Thursday · Connect · 2:00 · Option R: Our Room’s Own Marks

Draw one of the room’s marks at a time on the board, in random order. Students show or tell a partner what they would do. Eight quick rounds.

Look for

  • Fast, correct responses

If it breaks

  • Slow down and do two rounds together.

Support adult

  • Keep the room chart visible.

Language support

  • Showing is a full answer.

Visual: Arrow toward the room’s chart; sentence stem.

Thursday · Practice · 6:00 · Option S: Structured Marks

Choose the modelChoose a student likely to succeed who has not modeled yet this week. Their paper will be shown to the class, so ask quietly first.

Optional non-example (teacher plays it)Play a student who gets a mark and raises a hand to ask what it means, or ignores it. “What should I have done?” Take two answers. Then: “Now watch [Name].”

Run itThe model works on the practice Do Now. Walk a short lap, make a mark on the model’s paper using the four marks (a dot on a blank spot works well), and hold the paper up so the class can see it. The model shows what to do next.

Narrate: “[Name] got a dot. [Name] did not ask. [Name] started right there.”

Ask the class“What mark did [Name] get?” “What did [Name] do?”

If it is excellent

“Did you see that? [Name] did every step. That is what excellent looks like. Now it is time for all of us to show how excellent we can be.”

If it is partly right

“[Name] got some steps just right. Who saw the step to fix?” Take one answer. “[Name], one more time with that fix.” Then: “Now all of us.”

If it turns silly

Calm, one line, no audience: “Thank you. That is exactly why we practice.” Offer one redo: “[Name], show us the real one.” If the student says no or keeps playing: “Thanks. [Next student], show us.” Keep moving and follow up privately later with the usual correction steps. Then: “That is why we practice here first. Then we do it for real.”

Then everyonePractice Do Now for everyone, 2 minutes, with a real lap. Mark and move. No talking at the desk.

If the class rep breaks“Let’s get that one more time so it is easy tomorrow.” One redo. If it still breaks, practice only the one step that broke.

Look for

  • The model responds to the mark without asking
  • Every paper on the lap path gets a mark

Support adult

  • Walk the same lap path you will use every day.

Language support

  • The marks work without words.

Visual: Watch, teacher lap, everyone responds.

Thursday · Commit · 2:00

“Find your mark. What did it tell you? What did you do about it?” Ask two students to point to their mark and say what it means.

“Let’s do the whole First Five with our hands.” Full chant with motions: “Enter. Seat. Materials. Start Right Away. Keep Going!”

Look for

  • Students explain their mark

If it breaks

  • Show one sample mark and ask again.

Support adult

  • Praise students who responded to a mark without asking.

Language support

  • Pointing to the mark is a full answer.

Visual: All five step icons with the promise.

Thursday · Transition · 2:00

“From now on, every Do Now has laps and marks. Tomorrow is Friday Cypher, so there is no Morning Meeting. Bring your best First Five to every room.”

Look for

  • Students know marks start now

If it breaks

  • Point to the anchor chart.

Support adult

  • Run the first real lap at the next Do Now.

Language support

  • The lap icon means the teacher is coming.

Visual: Lap path icon on red.
