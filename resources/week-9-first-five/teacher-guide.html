Print toolkitsDaily lessons

1. Set up once. The first lesson that needs it opens a setup screen: how materials reach students, where the Do Now is, what students do when done, what they do when they need a supply, and, for K–4, which mark system the room uses. Choose the option that matches how the room already runs. The lesson shows only the matching slides, and the choices carry across the week on that computer.

2. Print the grade-band toolkit. Post the anchor chart before Thursday. K–4 rooms keeping their own mark system use the chart already on their wall.

3. Stuck has one answer in every room: do your best and keep going. Students do not raise hands for help during the Do Now; the teacher comes to them on a lap.

4. A classmate models each step, not the teacher. The teacher narrates what the model does, then asks the class to name what they saw. Choose the model deliberately and ask quietly before the lesson: Monday, a student who usually comes in smoothly; Tuesday, a different student; Wednesday, a student who has struggled to keep working, at a moment they are likely to succeed; Thursday, a student who has not modeled yet.

5. Never ask a student to model the wrong way. Students remember what they rehearse. When a non-example helps, the teacher plays it, students name what is off, and then a student models it right.

6. Every model ends with a bridge to the whole class, whichever way it goes. The exact bridge language is in each Practice note below.

7. Morning Meeting practice is rehearsal. The first real arrival, transition, or class change after the lesson is the live rep. LiveSchool points and the Brag Board count at the live rep.

8. Laps and marks. Walk the same path every time. Every mark is one stroke; mark and move, with no explanation at the desk. First laps are mostly checks and dots. Press the dot firm and dark. Grades 5–8 use the four marks in every room, including specials.

5. Practice · 6:00

On screen: Watch, Then Show Me

The student model

Choose the model: Before the lesson, quietly ask one student who usually comes in smoothly: “I am going to ask you to show the class how we come in. Ready?”

Optional non-example (teacher plays it): “Watch me come in. Find what is wrong.” Walk in slowly, wave and chat, wander past the seat, forget materials. “What did you see?” Take two answers. Then: “Now watch [Name] do it the right way.”

Run it: The model steps to the edge of the carpet or the door and comes in following this room’s path.

Narrate: “[Name] is coming in. Walking feet. Voice off. Straight to the seat. Things out. Hands still.”

Ask the class: “What did [Name]‘s feet do?” “What did [Name]‘s voice do?” “What did [Name] do first?”

If it is excellent“Did you see that? [Name] did every step. That is what excellent looks like. Now it is time for all of us to show how excellent we can be.”
If it is partly right“[Name] got some steps just right. Who saw the step to fix?” Take one answer. “[Name], one more time with that fix.” Then: “Now all of us.”
If it turns sillyCalm, one line, no audience: “Thank you. That is exactly why we practice.” Offer one redo: “[Name], show us the real one.” If the student says no or keeps playing: “Thanks. [Next student], show us.” Keep moving and follow up privately later with the usual correction steps. Then: “That is why we practice here first. Then we do it for real.”

Then everyone: One table at a time: “When I say go, come in, sit down, get your things. Go.” Narrate by name as it happens: “Ava is sitting down. Ava is ready.” Run each table once.

If the class rep breaks: “Let’s get that one more time so it is easy tomorrow.” One redo. If it still breaks, practice only the one step that broke.

Look for: The model does every step; The class names the steps in order; Every student completes a rep.

Access: Watching a classmate shows the routine without words. Pointing and showing count as full participation. Every slide reads aloud in English and Spanish (EN and ES buttons).

If it breaks: Echo it a second time, softer.

Access: The echo gives every student the words. Every slide reads aloud in English and Spanish (EN and ES buttons).

The student model

Choose the model: Choose a student likely to succeed who has not modeled yet this week. Their paper will be shown to the class, so ask quietly first.

Optional non-example (teacher plays it): Play a student who gets a mark and raises a hand to ask what it means, or ignores it. “What should I have done?” Take two answers. Then: “Now watch [Name].”

Run it: The model works on the practice Do Now. Walk a short lap, make a mark on the model’s paper using the room’s own marks (a dot on a blank spot works well), and hold the paper up so the class can see it. The model shows what to do next.

Narrate: “[Name] got a mark. [Name] did not ask. [Name] did what the mark says.”

Ask the class: “What mark did [Name] get?” “What did [Name] do?”

If it is excellent“Did you see that? [Name] did every step. That is what excellent looks like. Now it is time for all of us to show how excellent we can be.”
If it is partly right“[Name] got some steps just right. Who saw the step to fix?” Take one answer. “[Name], one more time with that fix.” Then: “Now all of us.”
If it turns sillyCalm, one line, no audience: “Thank you. That is exactly why we practice.” Offer one redo: “[Name], show us the real one.” If the student says no or keeps playing: “Thanks. [Next student], show us.” Keep moving and follow up privately later with the usual correction steps. Then: “That is why we practice here first. Then we do it for real.”

Then everyone: Practice Do Now for everyone, 2 minutes, with a real lap. Mark and move. No talking at the desk.

If the class rep breaks: “Let’s get that one more time so it is easy tomorrow.” One redo. If it still breaks, practice only the one step that broke.

Look for: The model responds to the mark without asking; Every paper on the lap path gets a mark.

Access: The marks work without words. Every slide reads aloud in English and Spanish (EN and ES buttons).

Look for: Students are curious about the marks; Students name one thing they saw in the video.

Access: The picture carries the idea. Every slide reads aloud in English and Spanish (EN and ES buttons).

2. Regulate · 1:00

On screen: Reset Your Body First

Script: “In for four, out for six. The breath out is the one that calms you. Three rounds.” Count aloud with students.

Look for: Breath out is longer than breath in; Room is quiet by the third round.

If it breaks: Restart the count once. Keep it calm and short.

Access: Fingers show the count. Every slide reads aloud in English and Spanish (EN and ES buttons).
