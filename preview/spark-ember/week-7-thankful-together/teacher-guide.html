Week 7 · Teacher delivery guide

November 16–20, 2026 has five school days. Morning Spark and Ember carry the same through-line as Morning Meeting: Thankful Together, gratitude as a skill students can see and hear. Spark keeps its fixed format rotation: Monday Check-In, Tuesday Picture of the Day, Wednesday Zoom In, Thursday Would You Rather, and Friday Wonder, followed by the Time for Friday Cypher slide.

Spark leads straight into Morning Meeting

Monday through Thursday Spark end on a hand-off slide that names that day’s Morning Meeting lesson (Notice the Good; Say Thank You Like You Mean It; Gratitude in Action; and Thankful for Our Community) with a button that opens your band’s Morning Meeting deck. Grades 2–3 rooms see two buttons: grade 2 uses the K–2 lesson and grade 3 uses the 3–5 lesson.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. The day’s IXL codes are on the slide, top right, from IXL Week 12 (November 16–20). Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45; on Friday, advance to Time for Friday Cypher and students report to the hallway once the announcement is made.

Use day keys 1–5 (Mon to Fri), arrows, bilingual read aloud, Repeat aloud for late arrivals, and Week view. Wednesday’s reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals.

The week’s through-line: a bar a day

Every reveal ends with a Chant of the Day (K–3) or Bar of the Day (4–8) on the day’s focus. Read it once, have the class echo it, and post it by the door. On Friday, the five lines become the class’s set for Friday Cypher. The scenes show gratitude in places students know: a door held for a friend, a thank-you in the lunch line, a strong thank-you note, a custodian’s work after dismissal, and a gratitude wall for school helpers.

Ember videos center gratitude and calm: Sesame Street, Daniel Tiger, and GoNoodle songs and stories for K–1, guided gratitude meditations for 2–5, and SoulPancake’s Class Act, The Mindful Movement, and teens showing gratitude to their school for 6–8. Each day’s reflection practices the day’s gratitude skill. Preview each clip before 2:05; if YouTube is blocked, the board switches to the breathing circle.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Equity sticks, talk moves, and LiveSchool

Every reveal follows Show, Think (ten silent seconds), Call (one or two equity sticks), Talk (30-second turn and talk if time allows). The daily keys below give the exact cold-call question and stem for each band. Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “What did your partner say?” Award LiveSchool points during the work period for gratitude students can see, said out loud: “Respectful: Amara said thank you and named what her partner did.” “Responsible: Dante put the supplies away without being asked.” Never award points for being quiet or finishing first.

Respect and privacy

Monday check-in slips stay private; the counselor collects them by 10:00 a.m. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. All week, students choose what they share: a thing, a place, an activity, or a person all count, and no one has to name family or explain what they have. Never ask what students are getting, eating, or doing over a holiday. If a share touches something hard, thank the student, continue, and follow up privately. Thank school helpers by role, never by name.

When behavior gets in the way

Correct privately and briefly, with proximity and the expectation: “Do Now first, then IXL.” Offer a frame instead of an explanation to a student who has not started. Do not hold a student out of the reveal or Morning Meeting to finish the Do Now, and do not use LiveSchool deductions for Level 1 behavior.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday November 16

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in. Reveal: “I’m glad you’re here.”

Reveal / key: This week we practice being thankful together. Turn to a friend and say, “Good morning! I’m glad you’re here.”

Chant of the day (on the reveal): “I look around and what do I see? Good things everywhere for you and me!” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; an adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior. Reveal: model turning to a friend with a smile.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Show me a smile. Who can say “I’m glad you’re here” to a friend?” Turn-and-talk stem: “I’m glad you’re here.” (or a wave and a smile)

Ember video bridge: Listen to the thank-you song and sway gently in your seat. Then draw. (Ellie Goulding Thank You Clouds Song)

Reflection: Draw one good thing you noticed today. I noticed ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.

Reveal / key: Reps can be small: notice three good things, give one specific thank-you, do one helping action. Do them daily and noticing the good gets easier. That is the training.

Bar of the day (on the reveal): “Notice, thank, and do: three reps a day, the good comes through.” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Collect reps on the board and carry the best ones into Friday Cypher.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What would your daily gratitude reps be?” Turn-and-talk stem: “My daily reps would be ___.”

IXL codes on the slide: Grade 4: Math 7BS · Reading 5Y6; Grade 5: Math 64E · Reading XGL.

Ember video bridge: Picture a garden of things you are thankful for. Rest, then write. (Guided Meditation for Gratitude)

Reflection: What gratitude rep will you keep doing next week? My gratitude rep will be ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.

Tuesday November 17

Spark task: It is 6:00 p.m. Make a claim: what work is happening here that most students never see? Argue whether a thank-you needs words.

Reveal / key: A custodian is mopping after dismissal so the hallway is clean tomorrow. The student stops and says, “Appreciate you.” Unseen work makes school run, and a thank-you can be words, a wave, or walking around the wet floor.

Bar of the day (on the reveal): “Awkward for a second, it stays for years. Say it like you mean it; let them hear.” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Push for evidence from the picture (time, mop, wet-floor sign). Accept either side of the words question with a reason.

Thursday November 19

Spark task: Would you rather thank someone in front of everyone or one on one? Defend your choice and the other side.

Reveal / key: A public shout-out lets the community see it. A private thank-you can feel more sincere for people who do not like the spotlight. Knowing your audience is part of gratitude.

Bar of the day (on the reveal): “Seen or unseen, the work is real. Make them feel seen; that’s the deal.” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Push for a counterargument. Connect to Friday Cypher: shout-outs are public gratitude.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Which did you choose, and what is the strongest argument for the other side?” Turn-and-talk stem: “I would rather ___ because ___. The other side would say ___.”

Friday November 20

Spark task: Wonder: artists shout out the people who put them on. Why do the best ones always give credit?
