Week 6 · Teacher delivery guide

November 9–13, 2026 has five school days. Morning Spark and Ember carry the same through-line as Morning Meeting: Reset and Return, the classroom response process every teacher now uses. Spark keeps its fixed format rotation: Monday Check-In, Tuesday Picture of the Day, Wednesday Zoom In, Thursday Would You Rather, and Friday Wonder, followed by the Time for Friday Cypher slide.

Spark leads straight into Morning Meeting

Monday through Thursday Spark end on a hand-off slide that names that day’s Morning Meeting lesson (What Gets in the Way of Learning; The Steps: Warning, Seat Change, Reflection; Reset and Write; and Tap In, Tap Out, and Coming Back) with a button that opens your band’s Morning Meeting deck. Grades 2–3 rooms see two buttons: grade 2 uses the K–2 lesson and grade 3 uses the 3–5 lesson.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. The day’s IXL codes are on the slide, top right, from IXL Week 11 (November 9–13). Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45; on Friday, advance to Time for Friday Cypher and students report to the hallway once the announcement is made.

Use day keys 1–5 (Mon to Fri), arrows, bilingual read aloud, Repeat aloud for late arrivals, and Week view. Wednesday’s reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals.

The week’s through-line: a bar a day

Every reveal ends with a Chant of the Day (K–3) or Bar of the Day (4–8) on the day’s focus. Read it once, have the class echo it, and post it by the door. On Friday, the five lines become the class’s set for Friday Cypher. The scenes show the process the way students will see it: a reset at the desk, the calm walk to Reset 1, lap marks on a paper, Tap In support walking in while the lesson keeps going, the reflection sheet, and the Reset and Return ladder.

Ember videos feature characters and voices students see themselves in: Sesame Street and Plaza Sésamo (including Spanish-language calm-downs) and GoNoodle en español for K–3, and guided practices from Big Life Journal, Mindful Schools, Kid President, and Headspace teachers for 4–8. Each day’s reflection practices a reset skill from the week. Preview each clip before 2:05; if YouTube is blocked, the board switches to the breathing circle.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Equity sticks, talk moves, and LiveSchool

Every reveal follows Show, Think (ten silent seconds), Call (one or two equity sticks), Talk (30-second turn and talk if time allows). The daily keys below give the exact cold-call question and stem for each band. Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “What did your partner say?” Award LiveSchool points during the work period for the resets students make, said out loud: “Responsible: Amara fixed it right after the reminder.” “Respectful: Dante moved to his new seat calmly and started working.” Never award points for being quiet or finishing first.

Respect and privacy

Monday check-in slips stay private; the counselor collects them by 10:00 a.m. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. All week, students talk about the scenes on the slides and their own resets, never a classmate’s behavior, a removal, or a Restorative Room visit. If a student names a classmate, redirect in one calm sentence (“We only talk about our own choices.”), continue, and follow up privately. Safety always comes first: any unsafe behavior gets immediate adult support.

When behavior gets in the way

Correct privately and briefly, with proximity and the expectation: “Do Now first, then IXL.” Offer a frame instead of an explanation to a student who has not started. Do not hold a student out of the reveal or Morning Meeting to finish the Do Now, and do not use LiveSchool deductions for Level 1 behavior.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday November 9

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in. Reveal: a ready body and “I’m ready.”

Reveal / key: This week we learn how to reset and come back to learning. Wave to a friend and say, “Good morning! I’m ready.”

Chant of the day (on the reveal): “Wait, breathe, raise my hand: learning keeps going, that’s the plan!” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; an adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior. Reveal: model a ready body: feet on the floor, hands still, eyes up.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Show me a ready body. Who can say “I’m ready” in a calm voice?” Turn-and-talk stem: “Good morning! I’m ready.” (or a wave)

Reflection: Draw what helps you wait for your turn. I wait by ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.
