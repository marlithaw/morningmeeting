Week 5 · Teacher delivery guide

November 2–6, 2026 has four school days: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. There is no school for students on Tuesday, November 3, so Tuesday’s Picture of the Day does not run this week. Morning Spark and Ember carry the same through-line as Morning Meeting: respect and responsibility. Spark keeps its fixed format rotation: Monday Check-In, Wednesday Zoom In, Thursday Would You Rather, and Friday Wonder, followed by the Time for Friday Cypher slide.

Spark leads straight into Morning Meeting

Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday Spark end on a hand-off slide that names that day’s Morning Meeting lesson (Respect Is Something You Do, Own It, Fix It, and Respect and Responsibility Everywhere) with a button that opens your band’s Morning Meeting deck. Grades 2–3 rooms see two buttons: grade 2 uses the K–2 lesson and grade 3 uses the 3–5 lesson.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. The day’s IXL codes are on the slide, top right, from IXL Week 10 (November 2–6). Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45; on Friday, advance to Time for Friday Cypher and students report to the hallway once the announcement is made.

Use day keys 1–4 (Mon, Wed, Thu, Fri), arrows, bilingual read aloud, Repeat aloud for late arrivals, and Week view. Wednesday’s reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Equity sticks, talk moves, and LiveSchool

Every reveal follows Show, Think (ten silent seconds), Call (one or two equity sticks), Talk (30-second turn and talk if time allows). The daily keys below give the exact cold-call question and stem for each band. Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “What did your partner say?” This is LiveSchool Store week: award points during the work period for the respectful and responsible choices students name, said out loud: “Respectful: Amara let her partner finish before she spoke.” “Responsible: Dante cleaned his spot without a reminder.” Never award points for being quiet or finishing first.

Respect and privacy

Monday check-in slips stay private; the counselor collects them by 10:00 a.m. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. All week, students talk about their own choices or the examples on the slides, never a classmate’s behavior. If a student names a classmate, redirect in one calm sentence (“We only talk about our own choices.”), continue, and follow up privately. Any report of teasing or bullying is handled under campus reporting procedures the same day.

When behavior gets in the way

Correct privately and briefly, with proximity and the expectation: “Do Now first, then IXL.” Offer a frame instead of an explanation to a student who has not started. Do not hold a student out of the reveal or Morning Meeting to finish the Do Now, and do not use LiveSchool deductions for Level 1 behavior.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday November 2

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in. Reveal: a respectful good morning.

Reveal / key: This week we practice respect and responsibility. Say good morning to someone near you, or wave.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; an adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior. Reveal: model a wave and a calm “good morning”; both count.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Show me a wave. Who can say good morning in a calm voice?” Turn-and-talk stem: “Good morning, ___!” (or a wave)

Ember video bridge: Breathe in and fill up like a balloon. Breathe out slowly. Stay in your seat. (Balloon)

Reflection: Draw one way you showed respect today. I showed respect when I ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.

Wednesday November 4

Spark task: What might this be? Look at the colors and shapes. Draw your guess.

Reveal / key: A friend holding the door! Holding the door helps friends walk in safely. That is respectful.

Differentiate / facilitate: Accept any guess with a clue (a hand, a red door). After the reveal, practice a “thank you” for the door holder together.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What clue did you see?” Turn-and-talk stem: “I see a ___.”

Ember video bridge: Rest like a starfish in your chair. Breathe slowly, then draw. (Starfish)

Reflection: Draw how you helped fix a mistake or a mess. I helped fix ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.

Thursday November 5

Spark task: Would you rather hold the door for a friend or say thank you when someone holds it for you? Point to your choice.

Reveal / key: Both are respectful! Holding the door and saying thank you both show respect.

Differentiate / facilitate: Both answers are right. Practice holding an imaginary door and saying “thank you” together.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Which one did you pick? Show me with your hand: A or B.” Turn-and-talk stem: “I pick ___.”

Ember video bridge: Breathe gently and let your body rest. You may close your eyes or look down. (Your Little Star)

Reflection: Draw a good choice you made in the hallway, at lunch, or at recess. At ___, I ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.

Friday November 6

Spark task: I wonder: one friend picks up trash. What will the next friend do? Draw what happens next.

Reveal / key: When one friend makes a good choice, other friends often copy it. You can start a good choice!

Differentiate / facilitate: Accept any drawing of a helpful choice. Act out picking up and passing on the good choice.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What will the next friend do?” Turn-and-talk stem: “The next friend will ___.”

Ember video bridge: Wiggle a little in your seat, then flop and rest. Then draw your picture. (Wiggleflop)

Thursday November 5

Differentiate / facilitate: Accept either side with a reason. Connect to LiveSchool Store week: points recognize choices, not volume.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Which did you choose, and what is your reason?” Turn-and-talk stem: “I would rather ___ because ___.”

Friday November 6

Spark task: Wonder: how could we find out whether one respectful act makes others more likely to act respectfully? Name what you would watch for and count.

Differentiate / facilitate: Look for a countable behavior and two constants (place, time). Note that one week is not proof.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What would you count, and what would you keep the same?” Turn-and-talk stem: “I would count ___ and keep ___ the same.”

Differentiate / facilitate: Push past “they get away with it” to habits and peer tone. Connect to Friday Cypher recognition.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “What is one reason, and one way a community can help?” Turn-and-talk stem: “One reason is ___. A community can ___.”
