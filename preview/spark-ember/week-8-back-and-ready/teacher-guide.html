Week 8 · Teacher delivery guide

November 30 to December 4, 2026 has five school days. Morning Spark and Ember carry the same through-line as Morning Meeting: Back and Ready, practicing routines after the break, focusing for Checkpoint 2, holding the same expectations in new rooms, and finishing strong. Monday is the first day back after the Thanksgiving break, and Checkpoint 2 testing begins Tuesday. Spark keeps its fixed format rotation: Monday Check-In, Tuesday Picture of the Day, Wednesday Zoom In, Thursday Would You Rather, and Friday Wonder, followed by the Time for Friday Cypher slide.

Spark leads straight into Morning Meeting

Monday through Thursday Spark end on a hand-off slide that names that day’s Morning Meeting lesson (Reset Our Expectations; Ready to Focus; New Rooms, Same Expectations; and Finish Strong) with a button that opens your band’s Morning Meeting deck. K–1 and grade 2 rooms also see the day’s Lee & Tee lesson, which K–2 rooms teach this week. Grades 2–3 rooms see two buttons: grade 2 uses the K–2 lesson and grade 3 uses the 3–5 lesson.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. The day’s IXL codes are on the slide, top right, from IXL Week 14 (November 30 to December 4). On testing days, follow the testing schedule; Spark still runs at arrival. Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45; on Friday, advance to Time for Friday Cypher and students report to the hallway once the announcement is made.

Use day keys 1–5 (Mon to Fri), arrows, bilingual read aloud, Repeat aloud for late arrivals, and Week view. Wednesday’s reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals.

The week’s through-line: a bar a day

Every reveal ends with a Chant of the Day (K–3) or Bar of the Day (4–8) on the day’s focus. Read it once, have the class echo it, and post it by the door. On Friday, the five lines become the class’s set for Friday Cypher. The scenes show the week students are living: a whisper walk past a testing room, a calm start at a testing desk, a test-day plan, a silent finish, and a routines poster by a new classroom door.

Ember videos center calm and focus for a testing week: Calm Classroom, Sesame Street, and a snowy mindfulness practice for K–1; GoNoodle Flow and Cosmic Kids Zen Den for 2–5; a circle-of-control clip and Bruno Mars with Elmo for 4–5; and Calm Classroom, Headspace, TED-Ed, and Yoga Ed. for 6–8. Each day’s reflection practices the day’s skill. Preview each clip before 2:05; if YouTube is blocked, the board switches to the breathing circle.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Equity sticks, talk moves, and LiveSchool

Every reveal follows Show, Think (ten silent seconds), Call (one or two equity sticks), Talk (30-second turn and talk if time allows). The daily keys below give the exact cold-call question and stem for each band. Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “What did your partner say?” Award LiveSchool points during the work period for routines students can see, said out loud: “Safe: Amara walked on the right the whole hallway.” “Responsible: Dante had his materials out before the timer started.” Never award points for being quiet or finishing first.

Respect and privacy

Monday check-in slips stay private; the counselor collects them by 10:00 a.m. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. All week, no one shares a test score, a goal number, or a result, and no student is used as the example of a routine done wrong. Test nerves are normal. If a share points to worry about testing, the changes, or anything at home, thank the student, continue, and follow up privately.

When behavior gets in the way

Correct privately and briefly, with proximity and the expectation: “Do Now first, then IXL.” Offer a frame instead of an explanation to a student who has not started. Do not hold a student out of the reveal or Morning Meeting to finish the Do Now, and do not use LiveSchool deductions for Level 1 behavior.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday November 30

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in. Reveal: walking feet and a quiet voice.

Reveal / key: Welcome back! We missed you. This week we practice our school routines. Show me walking feet and a quiet voice, right at your seat.

Chant of the day (on the reveal): “Walking feet and a quiet voice: back at school, I make the choice!” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; an adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior. Reveal: practice walking feet and a quiet voice together.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Show me walking feet at your seat. How does your body feel this morning?” Turn-and-talk stem: “My body feels ___.” (or point to a weather picture)

Ember video bridge: Breathe like a snowflake floating down, slow and calm in your seat. Then draw. (Snowflake Breathing)

Reflection: Draw yourself showing a school routine. I practiced ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.
