Week 8 · Teacher delivery guide

November 30 to December 4, 2026 has five school days. Morning Spark and Ember carry the same through-line as Morning Meeting: Cool Heads, Kind Words, how to handle a strong feeling before it becomes a hurtful word or an unsafe action. Monday is the first day back after the Thanksgiving break. Spark keeps its fixed format rotation: Monday Check-In, Tuesday Picture of the Day, Wednesday Zoom In, Thursday Would You Rather, and Friday Wonder, followed by the Time for Friday Cypher slide.

Spark leads straight into Morning Meeting

Monday through Thursday Spark end on a hand-off slide that names that day’s Morning Meeting lesson (Notice the Spark; Cool Down First; Talk It Out; and Safe Hands, Strong Choices) with a button that opens your band’s Morning Meeting deck. Grades 2–3 rooms see two buttons: grade 2 uses the K–2 lesson and grade 3 uses the 3–5 lesson.

Preserve the daily routines

Arrival at 7:15. Spark is a 3–5 minute independent task; then K–2 read and grades 3–8 use IXL. In the 2–3 deck, grade 2 reads and grade 3 uses IXL. The day’s IXL codes are on the slide, top right, from IXL Week 14 (November 30 to December 4). Announcements are around 7:37, followed by a reveal of two minutes or less. Morning Meeting starts at 7:45; on Friday, advance to Time for Friday Cypher and students report to the hallway once the announcement is made.

Use day keys 1–5 (Mon to Fri), arrows, bilingual read aloud, Repeat aloud for late arrivals, and Week view. Wednesday’s reveal zooms out before the answer appears; reduced-motion mode reveals immediately. Thursday votes start at zero; explanations matter more than vote totals.

The week’s through-line: a bar a day

Every reveal ends with a Chant of the Day (K–3) or Bar of the Day (4–8) on the day’s focus. Read it once, have the class echo it, and post it by the door. On Friday, the five lines become the class’s set for Friday Cypher. The scenes show calm in places students know: a fallen block tower, a bump in the hallway line, a cool-down plan on a desk, a group chat at lunch, and a Talk It Out table.

Ember videos center calm bodies and calm minds: Daniel Tiger, Sesame Street Monster Meditations, and Cosmic Kids for K–1, GoNoodle Flow and Cosmic Kids Zen Den for 2–5, a Child Mind Institute clip on big feelings for 4–5, and box breathing and belly breathing practices for 6–8, ending Friday with the hand model of the brain from Monday’s lesson. Each day’s reflection practices the day’s skill. Preview each clip before 2:05; if YouTube is blocked, the board switches to the breathing circle.

Ember: 2:05 seated pack-up, 2:08 watch and settle, 2:13 reflect, then seated waiting. Keep the existing release order and times on the rail. Chromebook stays closed on the desk until the group is called; return it to the numbered slot, then leave. No lining up. Use the board’s breathing fallback if streaming fails.

Equity sticks, talk moves, and LiveSchool

Every reveal follows Show, Think (ten silent seconds), Call (one or two equity sticks), Talk (30-second turn and talk if time allows). The daily keys below give the exact cold-call question and stem for each band. Talk moves: “Say more.” “Who can add on?” “What did your partner say?” Award LiveSchool points during the work period for calm students can see, said out loud: “Safe: Amara took a breath before she answered.” “Respectful: Dante disagreed with a calm voice and zero put-downs.” Never award points for being quiet or finishing first.

Respect and privacy

Monday check-in slips stay private; the counselor collects them by 10:00 a.m. Feelings and readiness are information, not behavior grades. All week, students talk about the skill, not about a real conflict: no one names a classmate they are upset with, retells a fight, or explains what happens at home. Anger is a normal feeling, and no one is shamed for it. If a share points to a current conflict or a safety concern, thank the student, continue, and follow up privately through the school’s support steps.

When behavior gets in the way

Correct privately and briefly, with proximity and the expectation: “Do Now first, then IXL.” Offer a frame instead of an explanation to a student who has not started. Do not hold a student out of the reveal or Morning Meeting to finish the Do Now, and do not use LiveSchool deductions for Level 1 behavior.

Grades K–1 · Daily teaching key

Monday November 30

Spark task: Weather pictures; private slip; optional adult check-in. Reveal: one slow breath, hand on heart.

Reveal / key: Welcome back! This week: Cool Heads, Kind Words. Everybody feels mad sometimes. Put your hand on your heart and take one slow breath with me.

Chant of the day (on the reveal): “Hot face, tight hands, I feel mad: I notice it, and that’s not bad!” Say it once, then the class echoes it.

Differentiate / facilitate: Privately point or circle; an adult can scribe. Do not interpret a feeling from behavior. Reveal: model one slow breath with a hand on the heart.

Equity stick (after 10 seconds of think time): “Show me one slow breath. How does your body feel this morning?” Turn-and-talk stem: “My body feels ___.” (or point to a weather picture)

Ember video bridge: Watch Katerina calm down, then take a deep breath and count to four in your seat. Then draw. (Katerina Gets Mad & Learns How to Calm Down)

Reflection: Draw how your body feels when you are mad. When I am mad, my body ___.

Look for: a concrete, observable choice the student made or will make. Reflections stay about the student’s own actions; no one names a classmate. If a student has no real example, the morning model is enough.
