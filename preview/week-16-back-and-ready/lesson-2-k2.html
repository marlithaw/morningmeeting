Matchbook Morning Meeting · Ready to Focus · Back and ReadyGrades K–2 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. Today many of our classmates take Checkpoint 2. We practice how to start calm, stay focused, and help everyone do their best. Today’s focus is Ready to Focus.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for today: nobody shares a score, a goal number, or a result. Nerves are normal, and everyone’s best effort counts.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes. Then read the third card aloud: “Calm and focused.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can use a quiet voice and calm body while others work and test.” Point to each picture and read its label. Have students repeat each label with a hand motion. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Keyla, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: How do Elmo and his friends act at school?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Time for School with Elmo & Friends!, Sesame Street). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Mateo, how do Elmo and his friends act at school?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “We walk past a room where big kids are taking a test.” Point to choice A, then choice B, and read each one. Say: “Think. Do not call out. Point when I say go.” Give ten seconds of think time, then say “Go.” Students point to the slide or hold up a Show-Me card. Equity stick: pull a stick and ask, “Marisol, which choice did you point to?” Then pull a second stick: “Tell us why.” Accept pointing, signing, or a whispered answer to a partner. Answer: choice A, “Use a whisper voice and quiet feet.” Name it: “A whisper voice helps everyone do their best.” Talk move: “Who can say it in their own words?”

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the My Voice Sizes page from the K–2 toolkit. Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step on a copy under the document camera, or hold up the page and point. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Respectful: “Marisol, you used a whisper voice the whole walk. That helps the big kids.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The child matches places to voice sizes and shows a whisper voice. Access: Kindergarten: point to each place while an adult shows the voice size. Grade 1: say the voice size for each place. Grade 2: write the voice size next to each picture.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Voice Sizes. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “Today in the hallway, my voice will be a whisper.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Let’s practice a whisper walk to our next spot.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If whisper practice turns into shouting for fun, freeze and model the whisper once more. Call on a student who is using a whisper voice to show the class.
