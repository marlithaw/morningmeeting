Finish StrongTerminamos con fuerza

Sometimes work feels hard and our body feels tired or upset. We can ask for a break the right way: raise a hand and say, “May I have a break?” After a short break, we come back and try again. That is how we finish strong.A veces el trabajo se siente difícil y el cuerpo se siente cansado o molesto. Podemos pedir un descanso de la manera correcta: levantar la mano y decir: “¿Puedo tomar un descanso?” Después de un descanso corto, regresamos e intentamos otra vez. Así terminamos con fuerza.

Matchbook Morning Meeting · Finish Strong · Back and ReadyGrades K–2 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. We are almost to the end of a big week. Today we practice finishing strong: steady effort all the way to the last question and the last minute. Today’s focus is Finish Strong.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for today: effort is what we celebrate. Nobody compares scores or speed.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes. Then read the third card aloud: “Strong to the finish.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can ask for a break the right way and keep trying.” Point to each picture and read its label. Have students repeat each label with a hand motion. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Nia, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: What little word helps us keep trying?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (Sesame Street: Power of Yet Lyric Video with Janelle Monáe, Sesame Street). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Mateo, what little word helps us keep trying?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “Your puzzle is so hard. You feel like throwing the pieces.” Point to choice A, then choice B, and read each one. Say: “Think. Do not call out. Point when I say go.” Give ten seconds of think time, then say “Go.” Students point to the slide or hold up a Show-Me card. Equity stick: pull a stick and ask, “Keyla, which choice did you point to?” Then pull a second stick: “Tell us why.” Accept pointing, signing, or a whispered answer to a partner. Answer: choice A, “May I have a break?” Name it: “A short break the right way helps us come back and keep trying.” Talk move: “Who can say it in their own words?”

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Break, Then Try Again page from the K–2 toolkit. Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step on a copy under the document camera, or hold up the page and point. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Responsible: “Nia, you raised your hand and asked the right way. That’s finishing strong.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The child colors the break steps and draws themself trying again. Access: Kindergarten: point to each step while an adult reads it. Grade 1: say the break words. Grade 2: write “May I have a break?” under the picture.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Keep Trying. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “When work is hard, I can ask for a break and then try again.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Today, if it gets hard, ask for a break, then try again.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If students ask for “breaks” as a game, keep it calm: “Breaks are for when we need them. Let’s practice the words once.” Model it and move on.
