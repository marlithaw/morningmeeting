Ready to FocusListos para enfocarnos

Nerves before a test are your body getting ready, not a sign you will fail. Slow breathing tells your body you are safe so your thinking brain can work. Pace yourself: skip and come back instead of freezing, and do not rush to be first. When you finish, check, then stay silent. Your classmates are still working.Los nervios antes de una prueba son tu cuerpo preparándose, no una señal de que vas a fallar. Respirar despacio le dice a tu cuerpo que estás a salvo para que tu cerebro pueda pensar. Lleva tu ritmo: salta una pregunta y regresa en lugar de congelarte, y no te apures por ser el primero. Cuando termines, revisa y quédate en silencio. Tus compañeros siguen trabajando.

Matchbook Morning Meeting · Ready to Focus · Back and ReadyGrades 6–8 · 08

Welcome · ready screen

Say: “Good morning. Today many of our classmates take Checkpoint 2. We practice how to start calm, stay focused, and help everyone do their best. Today’s focus is Ready to Focus.” Use the usual greeting. The recurring First 5 video may play before the timed meeting. Ground rule for today: nobody shares a score, a goal number, or a result. Nerves are normal, and everyone’s best effort counts.

Regulate · 2:00

Say: “Let’s get our bodies ready to learn together. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly.” Model three comfortable breaths. Students may breathe, look at the slide, or rest their hands; no one has to close their eyes. Then read the third card aloud: “Calm and focused.” Keep individual supports such as fidgets, seats, and visual schedules available as usual.

Orient · 2:00

Say: “Today’s goal: I can manage test nerves with a routine and protect the room’s focus until everyone is done.” Point to each picture and read its label. Ask students to read the goal with you. Check for understanding with an equity stick: “Camila, which picture shows something you could do today?” Accept pointing. Say: “Yes. We will practice that right now.”

Connect · Look · 2:00

Read the message on the slide aloud, then press the read-aloud button for Spanish if your class needs it. Before the clip, say: “Watch for this question: What is one way the video says to beat test anxiety?” Press “Watch 60 seconds.” The clip opens with captions on and stops at 1:00 (How To Beat Test Anxiety, Child Mind Institute). If the video does not load, read the message on the slide a second time and go straight to the question. After the clip, give five seconds of think time, then pull an equity stick: “Jordan, what is one way the video says to beat test anxiety?” Talk move: “Can you say more?” Then: “Who can add on to that?”

Connect · Share · 1:00

Read the situation aloud: “Camila finishes early and starts whispering to her neighbor. Across the room, Jordan is stuck on a hard passage and loses his focus every time he hears her.” Then read each question: How does Camila’s choice affect Jordan’s test? What should she do instead when she finishes? Give ten seconds of silent think time. Turn and talk: “Partner A answers the first question. Partner B answers the second. Go.” After 30 seconds, say “Switch.” Equity stick: pull two sticks. Ask Brianna, “What did your partner say?” Ask Isaiah, “Do you agree or want to add on? Say more.” Close with the slide line: “Finish, check, stay silent. Everyone deserves their best shot.” Strong response: A strong answer says Camila’s whispering pulls Jordan off the hard passage, so her choice hurts his score, not hers. When she finishes she should check her answers, then read silently or rest until time is called.

Practice · Do It · 3:00

Students use the Test-Day Game Plan page from the 6–8 toolkit. Read each step on the slide aloud and point to where it is on the page. Model the first step in one sentence, then release. 0:00–0:30 model. 0:30–2:30 students work; circulate and read the page aloud for anyone who wants it. 2:30–3:00 two students share. LiveSchool · Respectful: “Isaiah, you planned to stay silent after you finish so others can focus. That’s respect.” Award points for the behavior while circulating, not for finishing first. Look for: The student completes two rounds of slow breathing and writes a before, during, and after plan that includes a move for freezing and a silent finish. Access: Grade 6: build the plan with the chips. Grades 7–8: explain why pacing beats rushing on a long test. Accept oral responses.

Commit · 3:00

Say: “The bottom of your page is a take-home card: Game Plan. Tonight, ask your family the family chat question.” Read the family chat question and the frame aloud. Model: “If I freeze on a question, my move is to flag it, keep going, and come back with fresh eyes.” Give 90 seconds to finish the frame by drawing, writing, or telling a partner. Commitments stay private unless a student chooses to share. Pull one equity stick for a volunteer share; a student may pass by saying “I am still thinking.” Students fold the page and put it in their take-home folder.

Transition · 2:00

Say: “Head to testing calm and quiet. Two slow breaths before the first question.” Name the next class task and where to start. Use your established release cue. Behavior support: If students joke that the test “doesn’t count,” do not argue it: “It counts for you. Use your plan.” Keep the tone calm and confident and cold call the next stick.
